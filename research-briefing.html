When it is useful

You need to decide something. The evidence is the awkward part.

The answer exists, but nobody has a spare day to research it properly.

Useful evidence is scattered across government guidance, professional bodies, studies, datasets or technical sources.

Two apparently credible sources seem to disagree and somebody needs to work out why.

You need something concise enough to read but properly sourced enough to challenge.

A Google search produces plenty of material but very little confidence about what deserves weight.

You want the evidence, not a consultant quietly steering the research towards the answer they prefer.

What you get

A briefing that separates what we know from what we do not.

  • a concise answer to the agreed question;
  • the key findings;
  • the strongest relevant evidence found within the agreed source boundary;
  • important evidence that points the other way;
  • uncertainty, limitations and material gaps;
  • practical implications and questions to consider;
  • a source list so you can see where material claims came from.

If credible sources disagree, the disagreement stays in the briefing. If the evidence cannot support a confident answer, we say that too.

The standard brief

One question. Clear scope. Five working days.

Normally 1,500–3,000 words, excluding the source list.

Normally up to 20 material sources where proportionate to the question.

One agreed geography, timeframe and decision context.

Normally returned within 5 working days once the question and inputs are agreed.

If the question needs materially more research, specialist interpretation, primary interviews or a larger report, we tell you before anything is agreed.

Examples

The sort of question that fits.

Organisational decisions

  • What does current evidence say about moving to a four-day working week?
  • What are the strongest arguments for and against this operational change?
  • What current official and professional evidence should we consider before deciding?

Markets, funding and options

  • Which funding routes are realistically relevant to this type of project?
  • What does current evidence say about demand in this defined market?
  • What are the main evidenced options and material uncertainties around this decision?

What it is not

A research brief should not pretend to be something more important.

Not professional sign-off

Not legal advice, clinical advice, regulated financial advice, statutory audit, certification or another specialist judgement we are not competent to provide.

Not research theatre

Not an academic systematic review, not primary market research, not “every source ever published”, and not a report written to justify a conclusion you had already decided on.

Why not just Google it or ask AI?

Sometimes you should.

If the question is simple, low-stakes and the answer is easy to verify, spending £900 would be unnecessary.

This service is for the awkward questions where search produces too much material, sources disagree, authority matters, or somebody needs to check what is actually supported before a decision is made.

AI may help us search, compare or organise material where appropriate. It does not turn an unsourced answer into evidence.
