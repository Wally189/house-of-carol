Research Briefing

What happens during the research?

1. Define

Agree the exact question, who needs the answer and what decision it is meant to inform.

2. Find

Use the strongest relevant current sources rather than treating every search result equally.

3. Compare

Check what the evidence supports, where sources disagree and where the limits are.

4. Brief

Turn the evidence into a concise answer written for the decision.

5. Check

Quality-assure material claims, sources, uncertainty and the exact agreed question before handover.

What sort of question is a good fit?

Examples include questions about an organisational change, relevant funding routes, evidence of demand in a defined market, the strongest arguments for and against an operational decision, or the current official and professional evidence that should be considered before deciding.

The exact subject must be within House of Carol's competent scope.

What is outside the scope?

This is not legal advice, clinical advice, regulated financial advice, statutory audit or certification. It is not an academic systematic review, primary market research or a survey, and it is not a promise that every relevant source in existence has been found.

It is also not a document written to justify a conclusion you had already decided on. If the question needs regulated or specialist professional judgement, we should say so rather than pretending a research brief is enough.

Why not just Google it or ask AI?

Sometimes that is exactly what you should do.

If the question is simple, low-stakes and the answer is easy to verify, spending £900 would be unnecessary.

This service is for awkward questions where search produces too much material, sources disagree, authority matters, or somebody needs to check what is actually supported before a decision is made. AI may help House of Carol search, compare or organise material where appropriate. It does not turn an unsourced answer into evidence.

Is this the right service for our organisation?

The current buyer hypothesis is a small or medium UK organisation with one decision or question that matters, without the time or internal research capacity to find and reconcile the evidence properly.

Likely buyers include owners or managing directors, operations or strategy leads, governance or board-support leads, charity or VCSE decision-makers, communications, policy or research leads, and public-sector managers where the question is non-regulated and within House competence.

Genuine demand and willingness to pay remain unproven; this describes the intended fit, not established market acceptance.
