01 · One-Process Rescue

The small, fixed front door.

For one recurring admin or operational process that creates disproportionate chasing, retyping, memory, hand-offs or catch-up. It is the easiest way to test the House on a real problem.

02 · Full House Review

Somebody needs to walk the whole business.

Not because the business is failing. Often the opposite: it has grown, adapted or become more complicated while several inherited systems kept doing what they always did.

Business Repair Report

Strengths to protect. Friction to remove. Repairs in the right order.

The Review looks proportionately across the real customer and operating experience. It can include customer journey, operations, recurring admin, documents and information, digital tools, website/content, routine enquiry and sales administration, accessibility, control weaknesses and key-person dependency.

Every area is classified as a strength to protect, friction to remove, opportunity, specialist issue or simply not material. We then look for the root causes that cross several areas.

03 · The Fix

Put one repair
into practice.

A Rescue or Review may identify something that is perfectly possible to implement yourself. If you would rather the House help build the agreed low-risk change, The Fix is separately scoped.

THE WORKING BIT

Build the useful artefact. Test it. Hand it back.

  • templates and customer communications;
  • forms, trackers and checklists;
  • SOPs and hand-off packs;
  • folder, document and information structures;
  • simple workflows and low-risk configuration;
  • website content, hierarchy or experience improvements where within scope;
  • existing tools before new subscriptions; and
  • documentation, ownership and recovery information at handover.

No passwords by email. Consequential changes need appropriate access, testing, customer approval and backup/rollback where relevant.

04 · The Refit

When fixing one piece would leave the rest awkward.

The Refit is deliberately gated. It only exists where a prior diagnosis shows several connected repairs should be delivered together.

Several rooms. Still one job.

A Refit might connect an enquiry process, customer emails, website route and document hand-off because repairing only one of them would simply move the friction elsewhere.

Finite scope. Coherent releases. No six-month transformation fog.

Refit controls

The larger the repair, the clearer the edges.

  • defined outcome and acceptance criteria;
  • fixed or capped commercial terms agreed first;
  • one accountable House lead;
  • the smallest useful sequence of releases;
  • testing and rollback where material;
  • customer approval for consequential change;
  • editable handover and removal of temporary access; and
  • no invented result claims afterwards.

The Refit is not an active off-the-shelf package. It is used only where diagnosis, competence and customer value justify it.

How we choose

No service roulette.

The route should become clearer as we understand the problem, not more confusing because the House wants a larger invoice.

RULE 01

Use the smallest intervention that can solve the real problem.

If a £149 Rescue is enough, the job should stay a Rescue. If one process is actually a symptom of a business-wide design issue, the House should say that before taking money for the wrong scope.

RULE 02

Technology has to earn its place twice.

First it has to solve a diagnosed problem. Then it has to beat the simpler option after cost, effort, lock-in, privacy, recovery and the customer’s existing tools are considered.

RULE 03

The customer should be more capable after the repair, not more dependent.

House work should leave understandable processes, editable artefacts and control in the customer’s hands. Deliberate lock-in is not a business model we want.
