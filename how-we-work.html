Approach

You should know what is being done, why, and what counts as finished.

The details differ by service. The working discipline does not: establish the real situation, agree the boundary, do the defined work, test the important result and hand back something the customer can use.

The working method

Five questions keep the work grounded.

What is actually happening?

We inspect the current process, website, materials or evidence rather than designing around an assumed diagnosis.

What exactly are we trying to change or verify?

Outcome, scope, exclusions, dependencies, access, commercial terms and acceptance are made explicit before material work starts.

What is the smallest competent intervention?

We prefer proportionate changes and existing tools where they are sufficient. Complexity has to justify itself.

How will we know?

Important assertions are tested against the agreed requirement. Observation, reproduced fact, judgement and unknowns are not treated as the same thing.

Can the customer use the result?

Completion includes a usable deliverable, known limitations, appropriate handover and a clear close rather than merely declaring the work finished.

A green test proves the test passed. It does not prove reality has signed the paperwork.
House of Carol working principle

When evidence disagrees

The stronger evidence wins.

During testing of this website, automated responsive checks passed while a real Android handset exposed a poor mobile rendering. The real-device evidence overruled the automated PASS. The defect was corrected and the test method was changed.

1

Record the precise failure

What requirement or customer task did not work?

2

Trace the cause

Was the defect in truth, proposition, content, design, implementation or the test itself?

3

Correct the smallest root cause

Preserve functioning work; change what the evidence shows is defective.

4

Retest and keep the lesson

A useful correction improves both the artefact and the next decision.
