Approach

Clear before clever.

The method is deliberately understandable: establish reality, agree the boundary, do the smallest competent work, test what matters and hand back something useful.

The method

Five stages, one visible thread.

Understand the real problem

What happens now? Who is affected? What repeatedly goes wrong? What would a useful result look like?

Agree the boundary

Outcome, scope, exclusions, dependencies, access, price basis and acceptance are made clear before material work starts.

Do the work

Existing tools and simpler routes come first. Complexity has to earn its place.

Test the important path

We test against the agreed requirement and record material limits. A green check does not prove more than the test actually covered.

Hand back and close

You receive the result, evidence and enough explanation to use it. If something remains outside scope, it stays visible rather than being quietly absorbed into the project.

The process should be visible enough to trust, but never become the product.
House of Carol working principle
One outcome.

Every engagement is organised around the result the customer actually needs.

One boundary.

Scope changes are made consciously rather than letting work spread invisibly.

One handover.

Completion means a usable result and an understandable close, not merely “work done”.

When something goes wrong

Defects are traced, not decorated.

A problem is recorded against the requirement it failed, corrected at the smallest sensible point and retested. If a customer or proprietor rejects the result, the rejection becomes evidence about the failed decision — not an invitation to invent a whole new system.

1

Record the exact defect

What did not meet the required outcome?

2

Trace the decision

Which requirement, design, content or implementation choice caused it?

3

Correct and retest

Change the root cause, then repeat the relevant test.
