## 1. Why this rebuild exists

The current site is technically controlled and reasonably clear, but the visual and content result is not acceptable to the proprietor. The mobile evidence supplied on 31 August 2026 shows an austere, beige, editorial one-page treatment with repetitive oversized serif headings, long vertical scrolling, weak visual drama, limited hierarchy, no real sense of a premium House identity, and copy that reads like process documentation rather than a confident British commercial website.

This is not to be fixed by adding cards, gradients, slogans, random motion, AI copy, or another long single page. It is to be rebuilt as a coherent multi-page commercial website.

## 2. Design challenge supplied by Alan

The new site must be:

- unmistakably House of Carol;
- British in tone, not generic American SaaS/start-up copy;
- glamorous, polished, deliberate and visually memorable;
- psychologically comfortable to read and navigate;
- technically sophisticated without showing off technology for its own sake;
- genuinely multi-page;
- free from "card soup";
- free from internal AI/engine/process jargon;
- written as a real business speaking to real customers;
- accessible, fast, resilient and usable on mobile, keyboard and enlarged text;
- capable of supporting future products and income streams without another structural rebuild.

## 4. Why the previous result failed

The previous implementation optimised heavily for restraint, accessibility safety, semantic simplicity and internal commissioning caution. Those are valuable constraints, but they became the aesthetic rather than constraints on the aesthetic. The result confused "simple" with "plain", "serious" with "austere", and "clear" with "large text plus long vertical sections".

The supplied good-site references show the missing qualities: strong art direction, visual confidence, compositional variety, controlled photography/illustration, intentional typography, contrast between sections, hierarchy that changes by page purpose, and a sense that the business has a point of view.

The current site instead reads like a service manual converted directly into HTML. It also uses a single long home page to carry too many jobs. That is why it feels repetitive despite technically valid structure.

## 5. Authoritative standards and professional references

The rebuild must use these as constraints, not as a visual template:

1. **W3C WCAG 2.2 Recommendation** — https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/  
   Technical accessibility benchmark. Target WCAG 2.2 AA for the defined customer journeys.
2. **GOV.UK Design System accessibility guidance** — https://design-system.service.gov.uk/accessibility/  
   Useful evidence-based patterns for keyboard, zoom/reflow, semantics and robust public-service usability. Do not copy GOV.UK visual styling.
3. **GOV.UK Content Design / style principles** — https://www.gov.uk/guidance/content-design  
   Plain, user-centred British content discipline. Again: use the discipline, not government tone.
4. **web.dev Core Web Vitals** — https://web.dev/articles/vitals  
   Performance targets: LCP <= 2.5 s, INP <= 200 ms, CLS <= 0.1 at the 75th percentile when field data exists; use lab evidence before launch.
5. **ICO storage/access technologies and privacy guidance** — https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-the-use-of-storage-and-access-technologies/  
   Do not introduce non-essential cookies/tracking without an appropriate consent mechanism. Prefer no tracking during commissioning.

## 6. Visual direction

**Not:** beige minimalism, generic SaaS blue/purple, startup-gradient clichés, card grids, endless boxed panels, huge repeated serif statements, dashboard aesthetics, internal AI motifs, fake testimonials, fake client logos, American sales copy.

**Do:** an elegant British editorial-luxury identity with deep ink, luminous ivory, restrained jewel accents and warm metallic detail; large but varied typography; theatrical transitions between light and dark sections; generous whitespace; strong rule lines; carefully placed ornamental geometry; subtle motion that stops under `prefers-reduced-motion`; expressive navigation; image/illustration spaces that can later hold commissioned House artwork; visual rhythm that changes from page to page while remaining coherent.

Colour must be tested for contrast and emotional effect. Glamour comes from proportion, typography, spacing, texture and restraint — not neon clutter.

## 8. Project method and defect learning

Every material choice must be reconstructable through:

**Requirement → rationale → implementation → test → result → defect (if any) → correction → retest → owner reaction → lesson.**

If Alan rejects the candidate, the team must not answer with taste arguments or a fresh redesign from scratch. Record the exact objection, identify which requirement/rationale failed, trace the responsible design/content/implementation decision, correct the smallest root cause, and retest the affected journeys.
