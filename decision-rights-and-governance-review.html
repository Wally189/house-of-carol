Decision Rights & Governance Review

Make it clearer who decides what — and where a decision goes when it cannot be resolved.

For SMEs and charities where routine decisions keep escalating, several groups discuss the same issue, or authority has become difficult to see. We map the decisions in scope, show how ownership and escalation work now, and recommend a clearer route where the evidence supports change.

For: an SME or charity with an accountable sponsor who can explain both the formal arrangements and what happens in practice.

When decision-making gets stuck

The same problem can show up in several ways: routine matters rise to senior meetings, the same issue is discussed in more than one place, nobody is sure who owns the decision, or day-to-day practice no longer matches the documented arrangement.

The review focuses on the route around those decisions: who is involved, what authority already exists, where escalation occurs and which governance routines help or hinder a decision being made.

What changes

You receive a documented view of how the selected decisions currently move through the organisation, where ownership or escalation becomes unclear, and which changes are worth considering.

Where the evidence supports a different route, we set out a proportionate target state. We do not invent authority, rewrite your constitution or make the organisation's decisions for it.

What you receive

  • Current decision map: the material decision routes in scope and how they operate now.
  • Reserved and delegated decision-rights map: a practical view of the authority already documented in your arrangements.
  • Role and owner clarity: who is currently involved and where ownership becomes unclear.
  • Escalation and referral routes: how exceptions, conflicts and unresolved matters move onward.
  • Governance-routine review: where meetings or routines duplicate work, create unnecessary hand-offs or leave gaps.
  • Prioritised recommendations: focused changes addressing the most important ambiguity, duplication or delay.
  • Target decision route, where justified: a clearer future route based on the evidence in scope.
  • One factual and scope correction cycle: an opportunity to correct factual misunderstandings or agreed-scope errors before final handover.

How the review works

  1. Agree the boundary. We confirm the decisions, roles, governance routines and evidence that are in scope.
  2. Map the current reality. We trace how the selected decisions are meant to work and how they actually move through the organisation.
  3. Identify friction and ambiguity. We look for duplication, unclear ownership, unnecessary escalation, gaps and differences between formal authority and established practice.
  4. Develop proportionate recommendations. Where change is justified, we set out a clearer decision, ownership and escalation route without adding unnecessary governance.
  5. Review and hand over. You check the findings for factual accuracy, agreed corrections are made, and the final outputs are handed over.

What we need from you

We need one accountable organisational sponsor and enough current information to understand the selected decision routes properly.

That will normally include relevant governance or decision documents, examples of the recurring decisions in scope, the roles involved, and the regular meetings or routines through which those decisions pass. We also need someone who can explain where formal arrangements differ from day-to-day practice and review the findings for factual accuracy.

Good fit, boundaries and no-fit cases

This review is likely to fit if

  • recurring decisions move between several people or meetings without a clear owner;
  • matters escalate more often than they appear to need to;
  • the same issue is discussed in several places before anyone decides;
  • informal practice does not clearly match the documented arrangement; or
  • people repeatedly become uncertain about who should decide or what happens next.

This review is not the right service if

You need a legal opinion, company-law or charity-law advice, formal constitutional drafting, a determination of statutory powers, or formal legal or regulatory sign-off. It is also not a substitute for a company secretary, board secretary, governance officer, legal adviser or other specialist officeholder.

Where a question depends on legal or constitutional interpretation, that part is referred for competent specialist advice rather than decided by House of Carol.

Your organisation remains the decision-maker

We can map existing arrangements, distinguish documented authority from established practice and recommend a clearer route. We do not transfer, exercise or determine your organisation's formal decision-making authority.

What we do not promise

The review does not guarantee faster decisions, financial savings, legal compliance or a particular organisational outcome. Its purpose is narrower: a clear, evidence-based view of the decision routes in scope and practical recommendations for making ownership and escalation easier to understand.

Worked example

Illustrative example only — fictional organisation and scenario, not customer evidence.

Larkstone Community Housing Trust is a fictional housing charity. Routine operational decisions repeatedly escalate to senior meetings, while other material decisions are made informally. Staff cannot tell which matters are reserved, delegated or simply habitual.

A review might identify that formal delegations and actual practice do not line up, several groups discuss the same issue without a clear decision owner, and escalation is driven by uncertainty rather than consequence.

A proportionate target state could map the representative decisions and their routes, clarify reserved, delegated and escalated rights without inventing legal authority, and recommend a simpler governance rhythm tied to real decision needs.

This example shows how the service works. It is not a recorded customer result and does not claim measured benefits.
