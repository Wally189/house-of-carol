Decision Rights & Governance Review

Make it clearer who decides what — and where a decision goes when it cannot be resolved.

For SMEs and charities where routine decisions keep escalating, several groups discuss the same issue, or authority has become difficult to see. We map the decisions in scope, show how ownership and escalation work now, and recommend a clearer route where the evidence supports change.

For: an SME or charity with an accountable sponsor who can explain both the formal arrangements and what happens in practice.

The problem

When decision-making gets stuck

Routine matters rise to senior meetings because nobody is sure where authority sits.

The same issue is discussed in several places before anyone actually decides.

Formal delegations exist, but day-to-day practice no longer follows them clearly.

Escalation is driven by uncertainty rather than the consequence or importance of the decision.

The outcome

What changes

We make the current decision structure visible: who is involved, where authority sits, what gets escalated and where the route becomes unclear.

You receive a practical view of the decisions in scope and a focused set of recommendations where a clearer route is justified. We do not invent authority, rewrite your constitution or make the organisation's decisions for it.

The work

What you will receive

Current decision map

A clear map of the material decision routes in scope and how they operate now.

Decision and ownership map

A practical view of which decisions are currently reserved, delegated or escalated, together with the roles responsible for them.

Escalation and referral routes

Clear routes for exceptions, conflicts and matters that cannot be resolved at the normal level.

Governance-routine review

A review of the meetings and governance routines in scope, including duplication, unnecessary hand-offs and gaps in ownership.

Prioritised recommendations

A focused set of changes addressing the most important sources of ambiguity, duplication or delay.

Target decision route, where justified

Where the evidence supports change, a proportionate future route showing clearer ownership, escalation and governance arrangements.

One factual and scope correction cycle

An opportunity to correct factual misunderstandings or agreed-scope errors before final handover.

The process

How it works

1. Agree the boundary

We confirm the decisions, roles, governance routines and evidence that are in scope.

2. Map the current reality

We trace how the selected decisions are meant to work and how they actually move through the organisation.

3. Identify friction and ambiguity

We look for duplication, unclear ownership, unnecessary escalation, gaps and differences between formal authority and established practice.

4. Develop proportionate recommendations

Where change is justified, we set out a clearer decision, ownership and escalation route without adding unnecessary governance.

5. Review and hand over

You check the findings for factual accuracy, agreed corrections are made, and the final outputs are handed over.

Your part

What we need from you

We need one accountable organisational sponsor and enough current information to understand the selected decision routes properly.

  • Relevant governance or decision documents.
  • Representative recurring decisions in scope.
  • The roles, meetings and routines involved.
  • Someone who can explain where formal arrangements differ from day-to-day practice and review the findings for factual accuracy.

Fit and boundaries

Where this fits — and where it does not

A good fit

Recurring decisions move between several people or meetings without a clear owner, escalate more often than they appear to need to, or rely on informal practice that does not clearly match the documented arrangement.

Not the right service

If the issue is primarily a legal, constitutional or statutory-authority question, or if the organisation already has clear and consistently applied decision routes, this review is unlikely to add value.

Outside the standard review

Legal opinion, company-law or charity-law advice, formal constitutional drafting, statutory-power determination and formal legal or regulatory sign-off sit outside this service.

What needs to be in place?

One accountable sponsor and the current information needed to understand the agreed decision routes. We confirm the exact evidence needed before the review starts.

What is outside the scope?

Legal opinion, company-law or charity-law advice, formal constitutional drafting, statutory-power determination and formal legal or regulatory sign-off are outside scope.

This review is not a substitute for a company secretary, board secretary, governance officer, legal adviser or other specialist officeholder.

Is this the right service?

It is designed for organisations where recurring decisions are slowed by unclear ownership, repeated discussion, unnecessary escalation or a gap between formal arrangements and actual practice. It is not designed to resolve a legal or statutory-authority dispute.

What does House of Carol not promise?

The review does not guarantee faster decisions, financial savings, legal compliance or a particular organisational outcome.
