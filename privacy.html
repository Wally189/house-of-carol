Privacy & UK GDPR

Use less data. Keep it only when it earns its place.

House of Carol is designed around data minimisation. The current preview does not contain an enquiry form, analytics or House-added tracking.

Who is responsible

The controller is Alan Gallagher, sole trader, trading as House of Carol, based in Bristol, United Kingdom. The full service address is supplied on formal business correspondence and transaction documents where it is required; it is not published as general marketing information.

The current preview does not accept enquiries or payments and does not intentionally collect visitor input.

What information may be used when services are live

Normal information needed to handle an enquiry or job may include business-contact details, the organisation represented, correspondence, agreed scope and delivery information, invoice/payment records, feedback or complaints, and limited material contained in customer-supplied evidence where it cannot reasonably be removed.

For the current Quote-to-Cash Fix, relevant evidence may include examples of the customer's workflow, status information, templates, messages or redacted process material needed to understand and implement the agreed repair. House of Carol should collect only what is actually needed.

Customers will be asked not to send passwords, access credentials, health information, special-category personal data or unrelated third-party records. Evidence should be redacted wherever practical.

Why information may be used

Enquiries and pre-contract discussions

To respond to a request and decide whether House of Carol can provide the work. The lawful basis will normally be steps requested before entering a contract, or legitimate interests for ordinary business-to-business administration where appropriate.

Customer work

To agree scope, communicate, invoice, deliver the specifically agreed service, answer questions and deal with problems. The lawful basis will normally be contract for an individual contracting customer, or legitimate interests where a contact acts for an organisation.

Business, tax and compliance records

To meet legal obligations and maintain necessary business records.

Marketing

Any direct marketing will be separately checked against applicable UK GDPR and PECR rules. Objections to direct marketing will be respected.

Sharing, implementation access and technology

House of Carol may use proportionate suppliers for website hosting, email, document storage and business administration. The live enquiry and email stack will be named and checked before customer data is routed through it. Personal information is not sold.

House of Carol will not ask customers to send passwords. If agreed implementation work needs system access, the preferred routes are customer-executed or guided changes, or least-privilege access created through the relevant provider and removed when it is no longer required.

Identifiable customer evidence is kept out of AI-assisted analysis wherever practical through redaction or synthetic examples. House of Carol does not use solely automated decision-making that produces legal or similarly significant effects about individuals.

Case studies

Customer work is not turned into public proof merely because it would make a good story. A named or identifiable case study, quotation, testimonial or logo use requires separate permission. Synthetic demonstrations remain labelled as fictional until replaced by properly authorised real examples.

Retention

  • Tax and transaction records are retained for the period required by HMRC.
  • Working customer evidence is normally deleted within the period stated in the applicable customer terms/privacy information after final delivery and any included clarification, unless there is a documented reason to keep it longer.
  • Unsuccessful enquiries are normally deleted after six months unless needed for a dispute, legal obligation or another documented business reason.
  • A minimal suppression record may be retained when somebody opts out of marketing so that preference can be respected.

Your rights

Depending on the circumstances, people may have rights to access, correct, erase or restrict personal information, object to processing, and exercise other rights under UK data-protection law. Once the permanent House contact route is live, requests can be made through that route.

If a concern is not resolved, a complaint can also be made to the Information Commissioner’s Office.
