Fictional worked example

From “someone usually sorts it” to a clear client hand-off

An illustration of the difference between documenting a messy process and actually redesigning one recurring hand-off so another person can run it.

Illustrative only — not a real customer, case study or claimed result.

Northbridge Creative · fictional B2B service business

The situation

A new client said yes. The real process began in somebody’s head.

Sales sent a mixture of emails and notes to delivery. Account information arrived in different formats, ownership of the first client contact varied, and missing details were often noticed only when delivery tried to start.

The founder or an experienced manager could rescue the hand-off because they knew who to ask and what “normally” happened. Newer colleagues could not see the same route.

Illustrative current process
Client agrees
Sales sends notes
Delivery checks what is missing
Manager answers questions
Client is contacted again
Work starts

What the sprint would investigate

Where responsibility, information and exceptions become unclear.

The real start point

“Client agreed” was not enough. The process needed a defined trigger and a minimum set of information before hand-off.

Ownership

Sales, account management and delivery each assumed somebody else was checking different parts of the hand-off.

Information

The same core details appeared in several messages, but there was no agreed hand-off record that everybody could rely on.

Exceptions

Urgent starts, incomplete briefs and unusual commercial terms were handled through experience rather than an explicit route.

A clearer future process

One route, named ownership and an exception path.

The redesigned process does not need to be elaborate. The important change is that the minimum hand-off information, owner at each stage, decision points and exception route are visible.

Illustrative target process
Client agreement confirmed
Minimum hand-off record completed
Named delivery owner accepts hand-off
Missing information follows exception route
Client receives clear start communication
Delivery begins

What the customer could leave with

A process somebody else can understand and run.

If this were real work

The sprint ends with the improved method, not a dependency on House of Carol.

If the process exposed a wider customer-journey problem, the next useful work might be an end-to-end journey review. If the issue was simply one recurring process, the finished process map and SOP are the deliverable.
