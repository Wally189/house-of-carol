Standards

Trust is mostly boring things done properly.

House of Carol does not need to look enormous. It needs to be clear, reliable, honest and easy to deal with.

01

Say what is actually being sold.

Before money changes hands, the offer, price, important exclusions and next step should be understandable without decoding jargon.

02

Do not invent proof.

No fabricated testimonials, customer logos, savings, credentials, urgency or traction. Objective marketing claims need evidence.

03

Use the simplest sufficient tool.

A spreadsheet, manual step or bank transfer can be the right answer. Technology is useful when it improves the outcome, not when it decorates it.

04

Protect information and access.

Collect only what is needed, keep access proportionate and never ask customers to put passwords into ordinary messages or documents.

05

Test consequential work.

Important outputs should have acceptance criteria and a sensible quality check before they are handed over or published.

06

Keep a recovery route.

If an automation or AI service fails, critical work should still be recoverable through a documented manual or hybrid route where reasonably possible.

07

Learn from defects.

A mistake is not closed because somebody says they understand it. The control changes and the changed behaviour is tested.

08

Make the customer more capable.

Where the offer involves implementation or support, unnecessary dependency is a defect rather than a business model.
