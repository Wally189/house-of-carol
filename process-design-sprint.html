SOP & Process Design Sprint

One important process, properly sorted out.

If work keeps getting missed between people, done differently depending on who handles it, or pulled back to the owner because nobody is quite sure what happens next, the problem is often the process itself.

House of Carol takes one recurring operational process, works out how it really runs, clears up weak handoffs and responsibilities, and turns it into a practical way of working your team can follow.

Best suited to: owner-led agencies, consultancies and similar non-regulated service businesses in England and Wales where a recurring client-facing process has become harder to manage as the team grows.

Tell us about the process

Where a process starts to hurt

Does this sound familiar?

The work still gets done, but only because somebody keeps an eye on everything.

A new client says yes, but the handover into delivery varies each time.

Important information sits across emails, messages, spreadsheets and people’s memories.

Two colleagues describe the same process differently, and nobody is quite sure which version is right.

A new starter needs repeated explanation because the real method has never been made clear.

Something gets missed, a customer chases, or a manager has to step back in.

Growth or hiring makes an informal process that once felt harmless harder to sustain.

What changes

From a process people remember to a process people can run

We improve the process first, then document the improved way of working.

We start with what actually happens now, not what an old procedure says should happen.

The result is one agreed process boundary, clearer responsibilities, defined handoffs and decision points, a sensible route for important exceptions, and a practical operating method that can be handed to somebody else.

The aim is not to produce a handsome document describing the existing problem. It is to make the process clearer and more usable.

How the sprint works

Five practical steps

The process is bounded before the work begins.

1. Define

We agree the process, its start and end points, the process owner and what a useful result needs to achieve.

2. Understand

We hold a remote discovery session of up to 90 minutes and review the minimum useful evidence needed to understand how the process really works.

3. Redesign

We identify avoidable friction, unclear ownership, weak handoffs, decision points and important exceptions, then design a clearer way of working.

4. Document

We turn the agreed improved process into a practical process map and SOP.

5. Hand over

We take you through the result in a remote handover of up to 60 minutes, identify your next actions and make any included in-scope correction.

What needs to be in place?

You will need one named process owner who understands the work and can make or confirm process decisions.

We will need a representative explanation of what happens now, the minimum documents, screenshots or examples required to understand it, and information about important exceptions, failures and dependencies. We may also need brief written clarification while the work is under way.

You remain responsible for the accuracy of the information you provide and for implementing the finished process.

Please do not send passwords, credentials or unnecessary sensitive information. Where evidence contains information we do not need, we will ask for it to be redacted or anonymised.

What is outside the scope?

The sprint does not provide legal, regulatory, tax, safety, information-security or accreditation certification. It does not redesign regulated, safety-critical or other high-consequence processes.

It is also not the right service for organisation-wide transformation, multiple unrelated processes, live system implementation or ongoing implementation management.

Your organisation retains ownership of the process and the decision to implement it.

Is this the right service?

A good fit

This sprint works best when the process happens repeatedly, involves more than one person or role, and has become dependent on memory, chasing or informal workarounds.

It is particularly useful where people do the same work differently, handoffs create recurring delays or rework, delegation and onboarding are difficult, or a manager or founder keeps having to rescue the process.

Probably not a good fit

If the process is simple, stable and already understood well enough for one competent person to document it clearly, a £1,500 sprint is probably more than you need.

What does House of Carol not promise?

We do not promise particular savings, productivity gains or commercial results, and we do not present this sprint as legal, regulatory or other professional certification.

Can you give an example?

A client handover that depends on people remembering the gaps

Imagine a small service business where an accepted piece of client work moves from sales into delivery.

The customer information is partly in email, partly in a spreadsheet and partly known by the person who sold the work. Nobody is entirely sure who owns the handover, what must be checked before delivery starts, or what happens when information is missing.

The sprint would establish how that handover actually works today, identify the weak points, define clearer responsibilities and exception routes, and turn the improved method into one process map and practical SOP.

This is an illustration of the type of problem the service is designed for, not a customer case study.

What evidence supports the service?

This service has been developed and tested internally, but live customer results, proven savings and buyer-validated outcomes have not yet been established. The scope, price and boundaries are stated plainly so you can judge the service on what it actually provides.
