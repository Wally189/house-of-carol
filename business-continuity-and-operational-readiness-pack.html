When disruption stops being theoretical

Could you explain what happens if something important stops working?

A supplier fails. A key system is unavailable. Your premises cannot be used. A crucial member of staff is unexpectedly absent.

You know which work matters most, but it has never been explicitly prioritised.

Fallback arrangements exist, but they depend on individual knowledge rather than an agreed route.

Important services rely on suppliers, systems or people whose failure has not been worked through.

Recovery priorities are assumed rather than recorded.

Nobody is entirely sure who activates continuity arrangements or who needs to be told.

A continuity document exists, but nobody has a practical plan for testing or maintaining it.

What changes

From informal assumptions to a usable continuity baseline

The aim is not to produce a large manual. It is to make the important decisions visible before disruption forces them.

You get a clearer view of which services or functions matter most, how disruption affects them over time, the minimum level of operation you need to maintain, what those services depend on, the fallback arrangements available, who activates them, who needs to be told and what should recover first.

Your organisation remains responsible for its own risk appetite, acceptable disruption, recovery priorities, recovery targets and activation decisions. House of Carol helps make those decisions explicit and usable.

What you will receive

A practical continuity package

The work is bounded around the services and dependencies that matter most.

Critical services and disruption impact

A clear map of the services or functions that matter most, what happens when they stop and how the consequences develop over time.

Disruption tolerance and recovery target

A clear record of how long your organisation can tolerate interruption and the target recovery position, so priorities are explicit rather than assumed.

Minimum operation and dependencies

The minimum operating level needed while normal arrangements are unavailable, together with the material people, technology, suppliers, premises, information and communication routes needed to sustain it.

Fallback, activation and communication

Practical fallback arrangements alongside clear activation criteria, ownership, deputy or escalation routes and priority communication arrangements.

Recovery ownership and priorities

The intended recovery order, responsible owners and important interdependencies. Where technology is material, relevant backup and recovery assumptions are recorded with the evidence currently available.

Exercise and maintenance plan

A proportionate plan for testing the arrangements, including who owns the exercise, its purpose, intended timing or frequency and what should be reviewed afterwards, plus triggers for keeping the continuity baseline current.

Handover and actions

An action and referral list, structured handover and one round of factual or scope corrections.

How the engagement works

A clear route from essential work to practical fallback and recovery

We use the minimum information needed to understand the continuity problem and keep specialist work with the right specialists.

1. Define

We confirm the organisation, accountable continuity owner, critical services, material dependencies and any specialist requirements that may fall outside the standard engagement.

2. Assess

We work through the critical services and the effect of disruption over time, including the point at which interruption becomes unacceptable to your organisation.

3. Design

We map material dependencies, consider plausible disruption scenarios and work through minimum operation, fallback arrangements, activation, communication and recovery ownership.

4. Check

Where technology is important, we record the relevant backup and recovery assumptions and the evidence currently available. Gaps needing technical or specialist investigation are identified rather than guessed at.

5. Hand over

We prepare the exercise and maintenance plan, check the completed baseline for consistency and completeness, and hand it over for factual review and one included correction cycle.

What needs to be in place?

You will need reasonably current information about the services and dependencies in scope and any relevant contractual, regulatory or recovery requirements you already know apply.

We also need one accountable continuity owner, relevant technology or recovery owners or providers where known, someone able to confirm factual accuracy, an owner for future continuity exercises and timely decisions on your organisation's own disruption tolerance, recovery priorities and activation arrangements.

An initial enquiry should contain only enough information for us to understand the continuity problem and whether the service may fit.

Do not send passwords, multi-factor authentication (MFA) or recovery codes, encryption keys, privileged credentials, security secrets or unnecessary personal information through an ordinary enquiry.

What is outside the scope?

This is not ISO 22301 certification or full business continuity management system implementation, a formal audit or conformity assessment, statutory or emergency-service emergency planning, live incident management, specialist safety, security or cyber assurance, penetration testing, technical disaster-recovery engineering, independent backup-restoration testing, execution of your continuity exercise or business-interruption insurance advice.

Work may need specialist referral or separate scoping where regulated, statutory, safety-critical, security-critical, major cyber-incident or certification requirements arise, or where materially separate organisations or sites need independent treatment.

Is this the right service?

A good fit

This service is designed for a small organisation that wants a proportionate continuity baseline and can work within one accountable continuity owner, up to 5 critical services or functions, up to 10 material dependencies and remote delivery.

The standard offer is organisation-paid and for business purposes.

Probably not a good fit

If the continuity need is materially larger, highly regulated, safety-critical, security-critical, certification-led or requires technical disaster-recovery testing or live incident support, the standard engagement is not the right route.

Consumer or private-purpose purchase is outside the standard offer and would need separate assessment.

If the work grows beyond the agreed boundary, we stop and reconsider the scope rather than quietly expanding the engagement.

What does House of Carol not promise?

No continuity service can guarantee uninterrupted operations or achievement of a stated recovery time. Recovery targets and tolerable disruption are planning decisions owned by your organisation, not guarantees made by House of Carol.

What evidence supports the service?

This is a new service, so House of Carol does not yet claim live customer results or proven recovery outcomes.
