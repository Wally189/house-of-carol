Business Continuity & Operational Readiness Pack

Know what must keep working, what it depends on and what you will do when normal operations are disrupted.

A practical continuity service for small organisations that need a clear, proportionate plan around essential services, dependencies, fallback and recovery — without commissioning a full business continuity management system, certification exercise or formal audit.

For: one small organisation, one accountable continuity owner, up to 5 critical services or functions and up to 10 material dependencies.

Discuss continuity readiness

Could you explain what happens if something important stops working?

A supplier fails. A key system is unavailable. Your premises cannot be used. A crucial member of staff is unexpectedly absent.

Could you say which services must continue, how long disruption can reasonably be tolerated, what minimum level of operation is needed, what those services depend on, who decides when continuity arrangements are activated and what should recover first?

This service helps you answer those questions before you have to answer them under pressure.

What changes

You move from separate assumptions and informal arrangements to a practical continuity baseline that makes the important decisions visible.

You will have a clearer view of which services or functions matter most, how disruption affects them over time, the minimum level of operation you need to maintain, what they depend on, the fallback arrangements available, who activates them, who needs to be told, what should recover first and what still needs testing, evidence or specialist attention.

Your organisation remains responsible for its own risk appetite, acceptable disruption, recovery priorities, recovery targets and activation decisions.

What you receive

Critical services and disruption impact

A clear map of the services or functions that matter most, what happens when they stop and how the consequences develop over time. Your tolerable disruption and target recovery position are recorded so priorities are explicit rather than assumed.

Minimum operation and dependencies

The minimum operating level needed while normal arrangements are unavailable, together with the material people, technology, suppliers, premises, information and communication routes needed to sustain it.

Fallback, activation and communication

Practical fallback arrangements alongside clear activation criteria, ownership, deputy or escalation routes and priority communication arrangements.

Recovery priorities

The intended recovery order, responsible owners and important interdependencies. Where technology is material, relevant backup and recovery assumptions are recorded with the evidence currently available.

Exercise and maintenance plan

A proportionate plan for testing the arrangements, including who owns the exercise, its purpose, intended timing or frequency and what should be reviewed afterwards, plus triggers for keeping the continuity baseline current.

Handover and actions

An action and referral list, structured handover and one bounded factual or scope-correction cycle.

How it works

We begin by confirming the organisation, accountable continuity owner, services, dependencies and any specialist requirements that may fall outside the standard engagement.

We then work through the critical services and the effect of disruption over time. Your organisation decides its own acceptable level of disruption, recovery priorities and recovery targets; our role is to help make those decisions explicit and usable.

Next, we map material dependencies, consider plausible disruption scenarios and work through fallback arrangements, activation, communication and recovery ownership.

Where technology is important, we record the relevant backup and recovery assumptions and the evidence currently available. Gaps that need technical or specialist investigation are identified rather than guessed at.

Finally, we prepare the exercise and maintenance plan, check the completed baseline for consistency and completeness, and hand it over for your factual review.

What we need from you

You will need to provide reasonably current information about the services and dependencies in scope and any relevant contractual, regulatory or recovery requirements you already know apply.

We also need one accountable continuity owner, the relevant technology or recovery owners or providers where known, someone able to confirm factual accuracy, an owner for future continuity exercises and timely decisions on your organisation's own disruption tolerance, recovery priorities and activation arrangements.

An initial enquiry should contain only enough information for us to understand the continuity problem and whether the service may fit.

Do not send passwords, MFA or recovery codes, encryption keys, privileged credentials, security secrets or unnecessary personal information through an ordinary enquiry.

Is this a good fit?

The standard service is designed for a small organisation that wants a proportionate continuity baseline and can work within one accountable continuity owner, up to 5 critical services or functions, up to 10 material dependencies and remote delivery.

If the work grows beyond those boundaries, we stop and reconsider the scope rather than quietly expanding the engagement.

When this is not the right service

This is not ISO 22301 certification or full business continuity management system implementation, a formal audit or conformity assessment, statutory or emergency-service emergency planning, live incident management, specialist safety, security or cyber assurance, penetration testing, technical disaster-recovery engineering, independent backup-restoration testing, execution of your continuity exercise, business-interruption insurance advice, or a guarantee that operations will remain uninterrupted or that a particular recovery time will be achieved.

Work may also need specialist referral or separate scoping where regulated, statutory, safety-critical, security-critical, major cyber-incident or certification requirements arise, or where materially separate organisations or sites need independent treatment.

An illustrative example

Fictional example — not customer evidence

Bramble Row Printworks is a fictional small printing business relying on a small team, one production site, cloud order records and two specialist suppliers.

Its continuity arrangements have developed informally. Critical services are not explicitly prioritised, fallback arrangements depend on individual knowledge, and recovery priorities and test ownership are unclear.

A Business Continuity & Operational Readiness Pack would make those elements explicit: identify the critical services and dependencies, define minimum-operation fallback arrangements and recovery ownership, and create a proportionate exercise plan.

The example shows how the service is intended to work. It is not a real customer case and does not claim measured savings, performance improvements or customer results.

House of Carol does not currently claim live-customer outcome evidence for this service.
