Business Continuity & Operational Readiness Pack

Know what must keep working, what it depends on, and what you will do when normal operations are disrupted.

A practical continuity-readiness service for small organisations that need a clear, proportionate baseline without commissioning a full business continuity management system, certification exercise or formal audit.

For: one small organisation, one accountable continuity owner, up to 5 critical services or functions and up to 10 material dependencies.

Be ready before something goes wrong

A supplier fails. A key system goes down. Your premises become unavailable. A crucial member of staff is suddenly absent.

The important question is whether your organisation can clearly explain what absolutely needs to keep running, how disruption affects it over time, how long interruption can be tolerated, what minimum level of operation is needed, what each service depends on, who decides what happens next and what should recover first.

The Business Continuity & Operational Readiness Pack turns those questions into a bounded continuity baseline people can understand and use.

What you receive

Critical services and impact over time

A map of the services or functions that matter most, what happens if they stop, and how the consequences develop over time.

Tolerable disruption and recovery position

A recorded customer-owned maximum tolerable disruption and target recovery position, with priorities made explicit rather than implied.

Minimum operating capacity

The minimum viable operating levels and routes needed while normal arrangements are unavailable.

Dependency analysis

The material people, systems and technology, suppliers, premises or sites, information and communication routes on which critical services depend.

Light disruption-risk assessment

A proportionate review of plausible disruption scenarios, existing controls and material gaps rather than a formal audit.

Fallback arrangements

Practical continuity and fallback procedures for maintaining minimum operation when normal arrangements are unavailable.

Activation and communication

Clear activation criteria, activation ownership, deputy or escalation routes, priority audiences and a fallback communication route where appropriate.

Recovery ownership and priorities

A clear intended recovery order, responsible owners and important interdependencies between recovery activities.

Technology recovery assumptions

Relevant backup and recovery assumptions, named owner or provider where known, current restore-evidence status and specialist escalation points.

Test or exercise plan

A proportionate plan with a named customer exercise owner, purpose or scenario, planned timing or frequency, evidence to retain and post-exercise review route.

Review and maintenance triggers

Clear triggers for keeping the continuity baseline current when services, people, systems, suppliers, sites, obligations or evidence change.

Handover and correction

An action and referral list, structured acceptance review and one bounded factual or scope-correction cycle.

How the engagement works

We first confirm the organisation, continuity owner, critical services, material dependencies and any specialist triggers fit the standard scope.

We identify the critical services and outcomes, assess impact over agreed time bands, and help make explicit when disruption becomes operationally unacceptable. The organisation retains responsibility for its own risk appetite and maximum tolerable disruption.

We record the organisation's target recovery position within that tolerable window and define the minimum operating capacity needed while normal arrangements are unavailable.

We map material dependencies, review plausible disruption scenarios, define fallback routes, make activation and escalation ownership explicit, establish priority communication arrangements and record recovery priorities.

Where technology is a material dependency, we record relevant backup and recovery assumptions and the evidence currently available about restoration. An unverified backup or recovery claim is recorded as a gap, not treated as proven capability.

We then create a proportionate test or exercise plan and define how the baseline should be reviewed and maintained.

The completed baseline goes through internal quality review, handover, customer factual review and one bounded factual correction cycle.

What we need from you

The organisation provides accurate and reasonably current information about the services and dependencies in scope, relevant contractual or regulatory requirements it knows apply, and any known technology backup or recovery evidence.

It must name one accountable continuity owner, relevant technology or recovery owners or providers where known, someone able to review factual accuracy, and a customer owner for future continuity test or exercise activity.

The organisation retains responsibility for risk appetite, maximum tolerable disruption, recovery priorities, recovery targets and activation decisions. House of Carol does not make those decisions for it.

Remote delivery boundary

The standard engagement does not include physical site inspection, supplier audit, customer-system access, direct disaster-recovery or failover activity, independent backup-restoration testing or continuity exercise execution.

If those activities become necessary, the standard engagement pauses for referral, re-scoping or specialist involvement.

Safe information handling

An initial enquiry should contain only enough information to understand the continuity problem and whether the service may fit.

Do not send passwords, MFA codes, recovery codes, encryption keys, privileged credentials, security secrets or unnecessary personal information through an ordinary enquiry route.

Special-category personal data is not required for a normal enquiry. If sensitive evidence later becomes genuinely necessary, the appropriate information-handling route must be agreed before it is supplied.

How is acceptance handled?

The agreed baseline must clearly record the critical services, impact over time, tolerable disruption and recovery targets, minimum operating levels, material dependencies, fallback arrangements, activation criteria and ownership, priority communications, relevant technology assumptions and restore-evidence status, recovery priorities, exercise plan and owner, review triggers, assumptions, gaps and referrals.

One bounded factual or scope-correction cycle is included.

What counts as a scope change?

If the organisation introduces additional services, dependencies, sites, organisations or materially different requirements, the work pauses and the scope is reconsidered rather than silently expanded.

When is this not the right fit?

The standard engagement may need to be declined, paused, referred or separately scoped if there is no accountable continuity owner, necessary evidence is materially incomplete, more than 5 critical services or functions or 10 material dependencies are needed, materially separate organisations or sites require independent treatment, or statutory, regulated, safety, security, cyber-major-incident, certification, live-incident or technical testing requirements are triggered.

What is outside the scope?

Not ISO 22301 certification or full BCMS implementation. Not a formal audit or conformity assessment. Not emergency-service or statutory emergency planning. Not live incident management, specialist safety or security assurance, penetration testing, technical disaster-recovery engineering, independent backup-restoration testing, continuity exercise execution or business-interruption insurance advice.

What does House of Carol not promise?

No service can guarantee uninterrupted operations or achievement of a stated recovery time. Recovery targets and tolerable disruption are planning decisions owned by the organisation, not guarantees made by House of Carol.
