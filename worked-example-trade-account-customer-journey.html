WORKED EXAMPLE

A clearer route from trade-account application to first order

A simple illustration of what becomes visible when one customer journey is reviewed from beginning to end instead of step by step.

Illustrative example — shown to explain how the service can be applied. It is not a customer testimonial or measured result.

Situation

Every individual step makes some sense. Together, they are hard to follow.

Example situation: a trade-supply business has allowed its trade-account journey to grow over time. A prospective customer finds account information online, downloads an application, returns it separately, waits for review, receives a decision and then goes through another activation and account-setup process before ordering.

The issue is not that every control is wrong. It is that the customer has to piece together several connected activities as though they were separate processes.

Illustrative current journey
Find account information
Download application
Return it separately
Wait for review
Receive decision
Activate online
Complete account setup
Ready to order

What House of Carol would look at

Friction, hand-offs and controls in the same picture.

Channel changes

The journey moves between web content, a separate application route, communications and online activation. Each change is a place where context can be lost.

Disconnected stages

Application, decision and activation are closely connected for the customer but may behave like separate internal processes.

Waiting without enough visibility

Once the application enters review, the customer may have limited information about status, further requirements or what happens next.

Requirements discovered late

Additional users, delivery locations and purchasing controls may be identifiable earlier and routed to the right person.

Controls that still matter

Credit decisions, authorisations and higher-risk account changes still need accountable judgement. Making the journey clearer does not mean removing necessary control.

What a clearer state could look like

Make one connected service feel like one connected service.

A target journey could organise the experience around a single understandable route while leaving credit decisions, approvals and other necessary controls with the business.

Illustrative target journey
Understand the account
Apply through one route
Receive confirmation and reference
Application reviewed
Provide anything further
Receive decision
Activate securely
Ready to order

What might logically follow

The findings determine whether anything else is worth doing.

A process problem could move into the SOP & Process Design Sprint. A support-information problem could move into a knowledge-base build. An unfinished website journey could move into the Website Completion Sprint. Those are separate services, scoped only when the finding is clear. If the review itself resolves the decision need, nothing further is required.
