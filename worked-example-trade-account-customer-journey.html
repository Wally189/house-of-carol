Worked example · HOC-018

Making a complicated account-opening journey simple

How House of Carol can map a fragmented customer process, distinguish necessary controls from avoidable friction, and show a clearer way through.

Illustrative example only — fictional business and scenario, not customer evidence.

Alderwick Trade Supplies · fictional trade-supply business

The situation

One customer journey had become several separate activities

Alderwick Trade Supplies had a useful trade-account service, but the route from wanting an account to being ready to order had developed in stages.

Each individual step could have a reason. The difficulty appears when nobody looks at the whole route from the customer's point of view.

What feels like a series of internal tasks to the business can feel like one unnecessarily complicated process to the customer.

What House of Carol reviewed

Look across the whole route, not just the visible symptoms

The customer journey

What did someone need to understand, provide, do and wait for?

The hand-offs

Where did information or responsibility move between forms, people, systems or channels?

The controls

Which stages existed for a genuine operational reason and needed to remain?

The gaps

Where could the customer lose sight of what was happening or what came next?

The target journey

How could the route become easier to understand without removing necessary business judgement or control?

What the review found

Separate avoidable friction from controls that are there for a reason

Too many changes of channel

The journey moved between web content, a separate application, communications and account activation. Changing channel is not automatically wrong, but unnecessary hand-offs make a process harder to follow.

One journey behaved like several processes

Application, approval and online activation were closely connected from the customer's perspective but appeared as separate activities.

Limited visibility between stages

Once the application entered review, the customer had less clarity about where things stood, whether anything further was required and what would happen next.

Useful requirements appeared late

Additional users, delivery locations or purchasing controls could be identified earlier and routed to the appropriate person.

Some friction was useful

Credit decisions, authorisations and higher-risk changes still require accountable human judgement. The objective is not to automate everything.

Remove avoidable complexity. Keep the controls that genuinely matter.

A clearer target journey

Make the process understandable from beginning to end

The bounded target state reorganises the experience around one coherent route while leaving business decisions and necessary controls with the organisation.

The underlying business process can still be sophisticated. The customer experience does not have to feel sophisticated.

House of Carol does not take over the organisation's authority

Credit or eligibility decisions, business rules, approval authorities, legal or regulatory requirements, system choices, implementation decisions and final acceptance remain with the organisation.
