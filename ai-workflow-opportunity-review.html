AI Workflow Opportunity Review

Find the few places where AI is worth testing — and the places where it is not.

If several recurring tasks look as though AI might help, this review gives you a practical decision before you invest in implementation. We examine 3–5 workflows, the problems behind them and the conditions that would make an AI test useful or premature.

For: small UK B2B service organisations with one team or function and 3–5 recurring workflows to assess.

£900 fixed fee

One team or function · 3–5 recurring workflows · one 60-minute discovery session · written review · one 45-minute findings readout.

Planned delivery: normally within five working days once the required workflow information and discovery session are complete.

A decision review, not an implementation project

Discuss your workflows

What changes

Worth a small AI test. The problem is clear enough, the workflow is suitable and a bounded test can answer something useful.

Fix the process first. The main obstacle is an unclear or unstable way of working rather than a missing AI tool.

Use a simpler non-AI route. Ordinary software, clearer rules or process change looks more proportionate.

Hold or stop. Important evidence, controls or dependencies are missing, or AI is not a sensible fit for the workflow as currently defined.

What you will receive

Workflow discovery

A 60-minute remote session covering the agreed workflows, what they are trying to achieve, current friction, tools, information, approvals and dependencies.

Opportunity map

A concise decision view for each workflow showing the problem, evidence, assumptions, readiness, AI fit, human oversight and material dependencies.

Prioritised opportunities

Up to three opportunities where the evidence justifies further testing, ranked against the alternatives rather than padded to fill a list.

Smallest useful tests

A bounded next test for each shortlisted opportunity, including prerequisites and conditions for stopping or changing direction.

What to fix or leave alone

A clear view of workflows better handled through process repair, ordinary tools, more evidence or no AI work at this stage.

Findings readout

A 45-minute remote session to explain the recommendations and answer questions, plus one correction cycle for factual or scope errors.

Before anything is agreed, House of Carol checks that the organisation, workflows and information required fit the standard review.

What needs to be in place?

A named manager or process owner who can explain the workflows and make decisions about next steps.

Descriptions of 3–5 recurring workflows, including who performs and approves them, the tools currently used and any known information or control constraints. Synthetic, anonymised or appropriately redacted examples are preferred where examples are useful.

What is outside the scope?

The review does not build or configure AI tools or automations, procure software, provide legal advice or certification, or produce an organisation-wide AI strategy.

The standard review does not cover high-consequence clinical, safeguarding, employment, credit or eligibility decision systems.

Is this the right service?

It fits when you have several real recurring workflows and genuine uncertainty about where AI would be useful.

If the implementation requirement is already well defined, or the main problem is simply a broken process, a more focused service is likely to fit better.

What does House of Carol not promise?

The review does not guarantee time savings, productivity gains, revenue impact, implementation feasibility or a recommendation to use AI.
