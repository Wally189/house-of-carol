AI Workflow Opportunity Review

Work out where AI is actually worth testing — before you spend time or money implementing it.

If several parts of your business look as though AI might help, the difficult question is not “what can AI do?” It is “where would it genuinely improve the work, what needs fixing first, and where should we leave well alone?”

For: Small B2B service organisations in England and Wales with one team or business function and 3–5 recurring workflows they want to assess.

£900 fixed fee

The standard review covers one team or function, 3–5 recurring workflows, one 60-minute discovery session, a written opportunity review and one 45-minute findings readout.

Planned delivery: normally within five working days once the required workflow information and discovery session are complete.

No automatic recommendation to use AI · no implementation bundled into the review · no promised savings or ROI without evidence

What changes

You finish with a practical decision about each workflow — not a generic list of AI ideas.

TEST FIRST
A small, reversible AI test appears justified.

FIX OR STANDARDISE FIRST
The process needs repair before AI is sensible.

NON-AI OPTION FIRST
Ordinary tooling, rules or process change look more proportionate.

HOLD / EVIDENCE REQUIRED
An important dependency or unknown needs resolving first.

DO NOT PROGRESS WITH AI IN CURRENT SCOPE
The risk, consequence or fit does not justify an AI test.

Fewer than three AI opportunities — including none — is a valid result.

What you will receive

Workflow discovery

A 60-minute remote session covering the agreed 3–5 workflows, their purpose, current friction, tools, information, approvals and dependencies.

Workflow opportunity map

One decision card for each workflow showing the problem, evidence, assumptions, readiness, AI fit, human oversight, dependencies, material privacy, security or legal triggers and the recommended disposition.

Prioritised opportunities

Up to three opportunities only where the evidence merits them. The review does not pad the shortlist to make AI look useful.

Smallest next tests

A practical, bounded next test for each shortlisted opportunity, with prerequisites and stop conditions made explicit.

What not to automate

A clear list of workflows that should first be simplified, standardised, handled through a non-AI route, held pending evidence or not progressed with AI in the current scope.

Findings readout

A 45-minute remote session to explain the findings and answer questions, plus one bounded correction cycle for factual or scope defects.

What needs to be in place?

A named manager or process owner who can explain the workflows and make decisions about next steps.

Descriptions of 3–5 recurring workflows, including who performs and approves them and the tools currently used.

Known information categories and material dependencies. Where examples are useful, synthetic, anonymised or appropriately redacted material is the default.

No password, MFA code, API key, admin credential or unnecessary raw customer information is required for the standard review.

What is outside the scope?

The review does not build or configure AI tools or automations.

It does not procure software, perform a cyber audit or penetration test, provide legal advice, DPO services, DPIA sign-off, compliance certification or a regulated-sector professional opinion.

It does not provide an organisation-wide AI strategy or detailed ROI or business-case model.

It does not guarantee time savings, productivity gains, revenue impact or implementation feasibility.

The standard review does not cover clinical or patient-care decisions, safeguarding decisions, employment selection or dismissal, credit or eligibility decisions, or other high-consequence regulated decision systems.

If live sensitive data, privileged system access or a specialist or high-consequence question becomes necessary, the standard review pauses and the appropriate specialist route is required before continuing.

Is this the right review for our organisation?

It is designed for a small service organisation where:

there is a real team or function with 3–5 recurring workflows;

the organisation is genuinely unsure where AI is worth testing;

it wants an independent prioritisation before implementation spend;

the accountable manager can provide enough process information to make an evidence-honest assessment; and

the work does not require the review itself to make high-consequence regulated decisions.

You may not need this review if your own team already has a clear, evidence-based method for prioritising AI opportunities, if the implementation requirement is already defined, or if the main problem is simply a broken process that should be fixed without AI.
