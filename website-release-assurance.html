Website release assurance

A second pair of eyes before your customers become the test team.

For a small business, agency or organisation with a new website, redesign, campaign page or material update that needs an independent release check before or shortly after go-live.

What the service checks

The commissioning scope is up to 10 agreed public pages or templates and one primary user journey. We look for material problems that can make a finished-looking site fail in use.

That includes responsive behaviour, navigation, links and buttons, an agreed contact or conversion journey where safely testable, visual and content consistency, first-time-user friction, accessibility preflight, release-oriented SEO basics and observable public-surface privacy or security issues.

Typical triggerA launch, redesign, handover or significant website update.
Commissioning scopeUp to 10 agreed pages/templates and one primary journey.
Target turnaround2 working days after the required information and access are available, unless quoted otherwise.
RetestOne retest of up to 10 reported fixes submitted within 7 calendar days of the report.

What you receive

Findings you can reproduce and act on.

01

Prioritised issue register

Each material issue records where it occurs, the evidence, severity, why it matters and the recommended action.

02

Release recommendation

A concise executive conclusion: RELEASE, RELEASE WITH ACCEPTED EXCEPTIONS or HOLD.

03

Evidence classification

Observation, reproduced fact, authoritative external fact, inference and unknowns are kept distinct. Automated checks prove only what they actually test.

04

Bounded retest

Reported fixes within scope can be checked against the same path rather than assumed to work.

A real example from our own testing

Automated green did not mean mobile good.

While testing House of Carol's own site, automated responsive checks passed. A real Android handset then exposed broken mobile composition and headings wrapping badly. The real-device evidence overruled the synthetic PASS, the defect was corrected and real-device mobile inspection was added to the commissioning method.

Found

Rendered mobile experience failed despite automated QA.

The failure was visible to a real user and therefore mattered more than the comforting test result.

Changed

The method now requires a real Android handset.

The standard launch environment also includes desktop Chromium, keyboard traversal and enlarged-text/reflow challenge.

Lesson

A test result is evidence, not reality itself.

The release decision follows the strongest relevant evidence available.

What this is not

Assurance has boundaries.

This is not penetration testing, vulnerability scanning, security certification, a formal WCAG conformance audit, a legal accessibility opinion, exhaustive performance/load testing or comprehensive coverage of every browser and device.

It does not promise a defect-free website or a conversion result. Additional environments such as Safari/iOS, Firefox, assistive technology or other customer-specific conditions must be explicitly scoped rather than quietly implied.

Useful first information

Tell us what is changing and what absolutely must work.

The useful starting facts are the site or staging URL, intended audience, important pages, primary journey, known constraints and the person authorised to accept the report.
