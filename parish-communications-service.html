Discuss the service

What changes

The parish gains a repeatable production and approval route for the agreed temporal communications instead of relying on last-minute individual effort.

Facts, voice and final approval remain with the parish; House of Carol does not speak on behalf of competent Church authority.

What you will receive

Newsletter/web content

Customer-approved temporal copy for the agreed newsletter or web content types, grounded in parish-provided facts and sources.

Notices

Clear notices prepared from approved information, with competent parish authority retaining final approval and voice.

Information structure

A simple structure for where recurring facts, dates, notices and source material come from and who owns them.

Communications calendar

An agreed forward view of the in-scope communications rhythm once the service unit and capacity are defined.

Production workflow

A controlled draft, review, approval and handoff process so nothing is published merely because it has been written.

What needs to be in place?

Parish content owner.

Approval workflow.

What is outside the scope?

No speaking on behalf of competent Church authority.

No doctrinal invention.

Anything materially outside the agreed boundary becomes a separate decision rather than hidden expansion of the engagement.

Is this the right service?

The organisation fits the intended customer: Parishes.

There is an accountable customer owner able to provide the information and decisions the work depends on.

If the main need falls outside the stated deliverables or requires a specialist judgement House of Carol is not competent or authorised to provide, a different route is required.

What does House of Carol not promise?

The service does not promise a particular revenue, saving, ranking, award, funding decision, procurement outcome, productivity result or other business outcome unless that exact commitment is expressly part of the stated product — and none is implied here.

Customer decisions and competent professional, legal, regulatory or ecclesiastical authority remain with the appropriate owner.
