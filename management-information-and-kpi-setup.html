When reporting gives you numbers but not answers

You may already collect plenty of information and still find it difficult to tell what is actually happening.

Measures can accumulate because they are easy to count rather than because they support a decision. Definitions may differ between reports. Figures may arrive too late. Nobody may be quite sure which source is authoritative or who owns a measure.

The result is often more reporting without much more clarity.

What changes

We start with the recurring management questions you need to answer.

From there, we identify the information you already have, agree a small set of useful measures and define each one clearly. We then put those measures into a practical reporting structure with named sources, ownership, review rhythms and action triggers.

The aim is not another dashboard for its own sake. It is a manageable set of information that helps people decide what needs attention and what to do next.

What you will receive

The £1,250 service includes:

a management decision and information map;

5–8 agreed KPIs linked to real management questions;

a KPI dictionary explaining what each measure means, how it is calculated, its source, owner, review frequency and important limitations;

review of up to five existing systems or files;

a data and source map showing where the information comes from and material quality limitations;

one lightweight management reporting pack or template using suitable tools you already have, or a written specification where direct setup is not appropriate;

an agreed reporting rhythm, ownership and action triggers;

remote handover; and

one correction round for errors in the agreed work.

A KPI is simply a measure important enough to help you understand what is happening, spot something that needs attention and make a decision.

How it works

Start with the decisions

We establish the recurring questions management needs the reporting to answer. This keeps the work focused on information that supports decisions rather than numbers that are simply easy to collect.

Check what you already have

We review up to five existing systems or files.

We identify where useful information comes from, who owns it and whether there are material gaps, inconsistencies or reliability problems. Uncertainty in the underlying information is made visible rather than hidden.

Define the measures

We agree 5–8 useful measures and document them consistently.

Each measure has a clear purpose, definition, calculation logic, source, owner, review frequency and appropriate action or review trigger.

House of Carol does not invent targets, benchmarks, accounting treatments or source information.

Put them into a usable reporting structure

We create one lightweight reporting pack or template using suitable tools you already have. Where direct setup is not appropriate, we provide a clear written specification instead.

We then agree how the information should be maintained and reviewed and complete a remote handover.

What we need from you

We need one accountable person who can confirm the management questions the work should support and make decisions where definitions need to be agreed.

You will need to provide the relevant existing reports or information sources, identify their owners and explain any known data-quality issues. Where a measure involves finance or accounting, its definition and underlying information must be supplied or approved by you.

We use the minimum information and access needed for the work. We do not normally need passwords, authentication codes or privileged system access.

Is this a good fit?

This service is designed for a small organisation or coherent management team that already has useful information somewhere in its existing spreadsheets, systems or reports but needs a clearer way to turn it into management information.

It is particularly suitable where you can identify recurring decisions or questions, nominate an accountable management owner and keep the work within five existing information sources and 5–8 KPIs.

You may not need this service if your important measures are already consistently defined, have reliable sources and owners, and are routinely used to make the decisions you need to make.

When this is not the right service

This is not a full business-intelligence transformation, data-warehouse project or systems-integration exercise.

It does not include complex BI implementation, APIs or ETL, more than five sources, more than eight KPIs, ongoing managed reporting, statutory accounts, bookkeeping, audit, tax work, regulated financial advice or assurance that the underlying data is correct.

It is also not designed for individual employee productivity surveillance or work involving sensitive personal information or specialist regulated activity without separate assessment.

Anything outside the agreed £1,250 service is discussed and agreed separately rather than silently added to the work.

Illustrative example

Fictional example — not customer evidence

Cedarline Repairs is a fictional field-services SME. Its managers receive a weekly spreadsheet containing dozens of figures but still struggle to answer straightforward questions about backlog, job age, rework and cash-sensitive completion.

Several measures duplicate one another. Some management decisions have no defined measure at all. Sources and owners are unclear, so the same KPI can mean different things in different meetings.

For an organisation in that position, the target state would be a smaller set of 5–8 clearly defined measures, with known sources and owners, a lightweight reporting pack and clear triggers for when management needs to investigate or act.

This example is illustrative only. It is not a real customer, testimonial or recorded customer result.

Finance and accounting

This is a management-information service, not an accounting or financial-advice service.

It does not provide statutory accounts, bookkeeping, audit, tax calculation or advice, regulated financial advice, or verification of accounting records.

Measures involving finance or accounting can be included where you provide or approve the definition and the information they are based on.
