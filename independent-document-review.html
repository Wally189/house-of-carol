When it makes sense

This is for the document you cannot afford to wave through.

A proposal is going to an important client and the claims need to survive scrutiny.

A board or management paper is supporting a material decision and the evidence needs challenging.

A funding, grant or tender submission depends on facts, sources and conclusions being defensible.

A research-backed report or public document carries your organisation's name and credibility.

The author knows the material so well that another internal read is unlikely to challenge the underlying assumptions.

AI helped with the draft and the finished prose looks convincing enough to hide a weak source or unjustified conclusion.

What we check

The parts that can make a polished document fall apart.

  • whether important factual claims are supported;
  • whether statistics, quotations, dates and attributions stand up;
  • whether the source actually supports the wording used;
  • whether conclusions follow from the evidence;
  • hidden assumptions, contradictions and reasoning gaps;
  • claims that go further than the evidence allows;
  • important contrary evidence or uncertainty the document overlooks.

This is not proofreading. We are not mainly looking for commas or prettier sentences. We are looking for the things that could weaken the document when somebody relies on it.

What you get

A short route from finding the problem to fixing it.

1. Define

Agree the exact version, audience, intended use and material sources.

2. Check

Test the important facts, sources and evidence rather than assuming the draft is right.

3. Challenge

Look for weak reasoning, hidden assumptions, contradictions and overclaiming.

4. Prioritise

Provide a clear findings list showing what matters, why it matters and what to do next.

5. Re-check

One bounded re-check of the revised version while the agreed scope stays the same.

Who it is for

People who have to stand behind the document after it leaves their desk.

Likely fit

  • owners and managing directors signing off important client or funding material;
  • bid and proposal leads before a material submission;
  • operations, strategy or governance leads preparing decision papers;
  • research, communications or editorial leads responsible for factual reports;
  • small teams that need a genuinely independent second pair of eyes rather than another internal reread.
Probably not worth buying

If a competent colleague can check the document properly in an hour and the consequences of a mistake are small, use the colleague.

This is also not legal sign-off, clinical or safety-critical review, regulated financial advice, statutory audit or certification, penetration testing or full AI-system assurance.

If AI helped write it

Tell us. But AI is not what you are buying.

AI-assisted drafts can look finished while still containing weak sources, invented details or conclusions that do not quite follow. That makes independent checking useful.

But the service is the same whether AI wrote 5% or 95% of the first draft: we check the document you need to stand behind.

Why £750?

The fee only makes sense when the document matters.

The review is designed for work sitting in front of a larger commercial, funding, governance or reputational decision.

We do not claim a made-up return on investment. The question is simpler: if a material error survived, would the consequence be worth more than £750?

If the answer is no, this is probably the wrong service.

What we need from you

The final or near-final document, who will see it, what it is meant to achieve, the main source material, and confirmation that you are authorised to share it.

Do not send passwords, credentials or unnecessary personal or sensitive information.
