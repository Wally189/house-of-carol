Responsible AI Workplace Training

Help staff make better decisions about AI at work.

Generative AI can be useful, fast and convincing. It can also be wrong, inappropriate for the information involved, or used in ways that leave nobody clear who is responsible.

This live workplace session gives staff a practical way to decide when AI is useful, when something needs checking, and when to stop and escalate.

Up to 20 adult participants · 30-minute sponsor/context intake · one 90-minute live remote facilitated session · six work-like scenarios.

Preparation: normally for an agreed delivery date within five working days once the required sponsor/context information is complete, subject to scheduling.

Practical judgement · active scenarios · no accredited qualification, legal advice or regulated competence claim

Does this sound familiar?

People are already using ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini or other AI tools, but practice varies from person to person.

Staff are unsure what information is safe to use with which tools or accounts.

Managers keep answering the same questions about acceptable AI use.

People trust polished AI output more quickly than they would trust an ordinary draft.

You have guidance or an AI policy, but staff still need help applying it to real work.

What people will learn

Choose the right task

Decide whether AI is suitable for the task in front of them.

Handle information carefully

Recognise when data, confidentiality or account choice needs extra care.

Verify important outputs

Check important AI-assisted facts and outputs before relying on them.

Keep human responsibility

Keep accountable human judgement for decisions affecting people, money, safety or professional judgement.

Recognise suspicious instructions

Spot instructions inside retrieved or supplied content rather than treating them as authority.

Use the organisation's own route

Apply the organisation's rules and escalation routes instead of guessing.

What the standard session includes

For one organisation or defined business unit:

  • up to 20 adult participants;
  • a 30-minute sponsor/context intake;
  • one 90-minute live remote facilitated session;
  • examples tailored to your named tools and policy context;
  • six work-like scenarios with active learner decisions;
  • a concise judgement/comprehension check;
  • a takeaway responsible-AI workplace guide;
  • a short manager follow-up note on recurring questions or misunderstandings; and
  • one correction cycle for genuine material defects in the supplied training content.

The session uses synthetic or redacted examples. Participants are not asked to paste real sensitive customer, employee or business information into AI tools for the sake of training.

The £750 price is House of Carol's current first-test price hypothesis and remains subject to review against genuine buyer and delivery evidence.

How does the session work?

1. Start with real decisions

Start with the workplace decisions staff already face.

2. Build a simple routine

Use task, data, tool, human responsibility and verification.

3. Work through contrasts

Use contrasting examples rather than relying on a lecture.

4. Make decisions

Participants decide on realistic scenarios and explain why.

5. Change the facts

Material facts change so the answer cannot simply be memorised.

6. Finish with your route

Finish with the organisation's own escalation route and practical next-step reminders.

What do you need from us?

A named sponsor, the learner group, the AI tools/accounts that matter, any current AI/data/security rules you want reflected, relevant customer or sector restrictions, and a route for questions that belong with your own Privacy, Legal, HR, Security, IT or professional owner.

What is outside the scope?

This service is not legal advice or compliance certification, a statutory DPO service, an accredited qualification or professional competence assessment, a technical cyber-security test, a policy-writing project, role-specific hands-on AI workflow training, leadership strategy/governance consulting, or a guarantee that one session will change behaviour, improve productivity or eliminate AI risk.

Is this a good fit?

A good fit is an adult workforce already using or about to use generative AI, where management can explain the organisational boundary staff should work within and needs a practical responsible-use baseline for ordinary work.

It is not a good fit where you need regulated professional training, formal certification, direct under-18 delivery, technical security testing, legal determination, a full AI-management programme or deep workflow training for a particular role.
