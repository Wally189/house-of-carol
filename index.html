    The Workbench

    A finished job should not have to find its own invoice.

    This is a clearly labelled fictional demonstration. It shows the judgement and implementation logic without pretending House of Carol has customer results it has not yet earned.

    6 daysfictional delay before invoice
    Finished Tuesday · invoiced Monday · demonstration

    The work is done. Nobody owns the next state.

    A service job finishes on Tuesday. The person who delivered it emails the owner when they remember. The owner checks the quote, retypes customer/job details into the accounting package and raises the invoice the following Monday.

    • “finished” is not a shared billable status;
    • the hand-off is an email rather than a controlled trigger;
    • quote/job details are reconstructed at invoice time; and
    • nobody can see exceptions without asking.
    1 gapready-to-invoice state
    The breakpoint

    The problem is upstream of payment chasing.

    The accounting software can send an invoice perfectly well. The business has not designed the moment at which completed work becomes authorised and ready to invoice, or made the minimum invoice information reliably available at that point.

    Buying another invoicing app would not create that missing commercial hand-off.

    1 statevisible ownership
    Better state

    Make billable work explicit. Move the information once. Surface exceptions.

    The repaired workflow defines a clear ready-to-invoice trigger, names its owner, carries the minimum customer/job information forward from the existing source and makes outstanding exceptions visible. Existing accounting-package reminders handle routine follow-up where appropriate; humans deal with genuine exceptions.

    This may remove avoidable delay and chasing. It does not guarantee when any customer will actually pay.

    House Notes

    Useful thinking around the work.

    The House will publish less, but better: commercial workflow observations that help an owner see the problem before anybody sells them another platform.

    NOTE 001

    A late invoice can begin before the invoice exists.

    If completed work has no clear billable state, invoice delay is built into the operating process. Payment chasing tackles the symptom downstream; the first repair may belong much earlier.

    NOTE 002

    Accounting software is not the owner of your hand-offs.

    Good software can automate defined work. It cannot decide what “ready to invoice” means for your business if nobody has designed that state in the first place.

    NOTE 003

    Before integrating two systems, ask what information deserves to cross.

    Moving every field automatically can preserve unnecessary complexity at machine speed. The House starts with the minimum information the next step actually needs.
