What we do

Four kinds of problem we are set up to tackle.

We keep the work bounded. If the problem needs a regulated professional, specialist certification or something outside our competence, we say so.

01

Website release checks

Before a site goes live, we check the journeys that matter on real screens, record reproducible defects and retest the fixes. Useful for agencies and small teams before handover or launch.

02

Data clean-up and structure

Supplier files, awkward PDFs, inconsistent catalogues and spreadsheet data can be turned into something cleaner and usable, with exceptions shown rather than guessed. Useful when accuracy matters more than volume.

03

Workflow fixes

If leads, jobs or information are disappearing between forms, inboxes and spreadsheets, we map the actual problem and fix the simplest thing that will work. Useful before anybody buys another system.

04

Research for a decision

We research a defined question, show the evidence, surface what could change the conclusion and stop when the decision is sufficiently informed. Useful when you need an answer, not a 40-page internet summary.

What the work looks like

A finding should be useful on its own.

For example, a release check should not hand you a coloured dashboard and make you decode it. A useful finding tells you what happened, why it matters and what to do next.

Finding

The contact button disappears below the mobile navigation at 200% text size.

Impact

A customer can read the page but cannot reach the enquiry route without changing their settings.

Evidence

Reproduced at the agreed viewport and text setting. Screenshot and steps recorded.

Action

Fix before release. Retest the same journey after the navigation change.

How we work

No mystery methodology.

Understand the problem

What is happening now? What needs to be different? Who is affected?

Agree the boundary

Scope, evidence, constraints, price basis and acceptance are made clear before the work becomes larger than it needs to be.

Do the work

We use the simplest competent route first. Automation is useful when it improves the result, not because it exists.

Show what changed

We separate what passed, what still needs work and what remains uncertain.
