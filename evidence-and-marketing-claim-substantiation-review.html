Evidence & Marketing Claim Substantiation Review

Challenge customer-facing claims against the evidence before release.

For SMEs and charities that want to know which factual, comparative or performance claims the available evidence can genuinely support — and where the wording needs to change.

£1,000 standard · indicative scope band £750–£1,500

Standard scope: one intended customer-facing use or version, up to 15 material claims and up to 20 material evidence or source items.

Remote · normally planned around 5 working days once the claim set is frozen and the evidence bundle is complete · timing confirmed during scoping

Discuss the review

When the wording is stronger than the proof

A claim does not have to be obviously false to create a problem. It may rely on a weak source, leave out an important qualification, use a comparison with no clear basis, or turn an aspiration into a statement of fact.

This review separates each material claim from the evidence behind it so you can see what is supported, what needs qualification, what needs specialist referral and what should not be used on the evidence available.

What changes

You move from a page, proposal or campaign containing untested assertions to a claim-by-claim view of the wording, the evidence behind it and the action needed before release.

The review does not manufacture proof or give a legal rubber stamp. It makes the evidence position and remaining limits visible so the organisation can make its own publication decision.

What you receive

Claim and evidence register

The material claims in scope, matched to the evidence and source items supplied for them.

Substantiation status

Each claim is returned as supported, supported with qualification, specialist referral required, or insufficient — do not use.

Qualifications and corrections

The limitations that need to stay visible and the smallest corrections needed where the evidence does not support the wording as written.

Residual-limit note

A clear record of what the review cannot establish, including material evidence gaps or specialist questions that remain open.

One bounded factual correction cycle

A correction round for factual or review-misreading issues within the agreed scope.

How it works

1. Fix the review boundary

Agree the intended customer-facing use or version and freeze the material claim set for review.

2. Supply the evidence

Provide the underlying evidence and source material you want relied on for those claims.

3. Test claim against evidence

We classify the claim, examine the basis supplied and identify whether qualification or specialist referral is needed.

4. Receive the findings

You receive the register, statuses, qualifications, correction recommendations and residual limits.

5. Correct factual misreadings

One bounded factual or review-misreading correction cycle is included before the review closes.

What we need from you

  • A clear intended claim set and the customer-facing use or version it belongs to.
  • The underlying evidence and source material you want the review to rely on.
  • A frozen claim set and complete evidence bundle if the five-working-day planning basis is to apply.
  • Your own approval for any final wording and publication decision.

Good fit, no fit and boundaries

Good fit

An SME or charity preparing factual, comparative, performance or other evidence-dependent customer-facing claims and wanting an evidence-honest challenge before release.

No fit

This is not the right service if you need legal advertising clearance, regulator approval, formal audit or certification, a legal opinion, a guarantee of regulator acceptance, or somebody to invent evidence that does not exist.

Specialist claims

Legal, regulatory, compliance, security, accessibility and certification claims may require referral to the competent specialist rather than an ordinary evidence-review conclusion.

Who decides what gets published?

You retain authority for the claim, its final wording, publication and any legal or specialist sign-off required for your circumstances.

A worked example

Illustrative example only — fictional organisation and scenario, not customer evidence.

A fictional energy-advice firm is preparing a service page that says it is “faster”, “more accurate” and “proven to cut admin time”. The team has some internal evidence, but it has not matched each claim to a defensible source or qualification.

The review finds that one quantified outcome rests on too small and informal an evidence set, while the comparative wording lacks a defined comparator. The practical result is not a certificate of approval: it is a clearer set of supported, qualified, referred or do-not-use claims before release.
