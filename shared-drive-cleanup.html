A fixed-fee review for a shared document area that has become difficult to trust.

The problem

When the shared drive stops being dependable

Shared drives rarely become a mess all at once.

A new folder appears because somebody cannot find the old one. Another copy is made “just in case”. Projects finish but never quite get archived. Nobody wants to delete anything because nobody is certain what is still needed.

People regularly ask where documents live.

Several copies appear to be “the latest”.

Old and current work are mixed together.

The folder structure has grown around individual habits.

New starters need a long explanation of where things are hidden.

Nobody is quite sure who owns the structure or decides what is current.

The outcome

What changes

The aim is not to produce a prettier folder tree. It is to make the important decisions clear: where current information should live, who owns it, how versions should be handled and what should happen to material that is duplicated, stale or uncertain.

You leave with a simpler target structure and a sequenced plan for moving from the current mess to a more dependable shared area without treating deletion as the default answer.

The work

What you will receive

Current-state map

The main areas, owners and users, plus duplicate, stale, ambiguous and hard-to-find material.

Source-of-truth and ownership decisions

Clear decisions about what should be authoritative and who is responsible.

Target structure

A simpler proposed folder or library structure with practical naming, version and archive rules.

A keep / move / archive / review plan.

A non-destructive sequence, including the decisions and stop points that need customer approval.

Retrieval map

A simple guide to where common information should live and how people should recognise the current copy.

Implementation checklist and handover

Ordered actions to put the plan into practice, followed by a remote handover session of up to 45 minutes.

The process

How it works

1. Define the area

We confirm the one team or function, one shared document area and one primary platform that the review will cover.

2. Understand what exists

We use a folder/file inventory, screenshots or directory listing where that is enough. Safe read-only access is used only where it is necessary and agreed.

3. Design the fix

We map the current problems, define source-of-truth and ownership decisions, design the target structure and set out the cleanup sequence.

4. Hand over the plan

We take you through the recommendations, clarify the decisions you need to make and leave you with the implementation checklist.

Your part

What we need from you

  • A named owner or decision-maker for the document area.
  • A discovery session of up to 90 minutes about how the area is used and where the problems occur.
  • Safe read-only access where appropriate, or a sufficient folder/file inventory, screenshots or directory listing.
  • Any existing naming, ownership, retention or archive rules.
  • Known constraints, sensitive areas or problem folders that need particular care.

We do not need to read every document simply because it exists. Decisions about the business meaning of files — including whether something should ultimately be retained, archived or deleted — remain with the customer.

Fit and boundaries

Where this fits — and where it does not

A good fit

This review is a good fit when one shared area has become difficult to navigate, several copies compete to be current, ownership is unclear, old and live work are mixed together, or previous tidy-ups have not fixed the underlying problem.

Probably not worth buying

If everyone can reliably find the current version, ownership is clear and the problem is mostly that you dislike the folder names, keep your £1,250.

Outside the standard review

Organisation-wide information-governance programmes, multiple systems, bulk file migration, destructive deletion, legal advice on retention, records-management certification, forensic discovery, eDiscovery, penetration testing and recovery of inaccessible or deleted files are not included.

What needs to be in place?

A named owner for the document area, a short discovery session, enough safe evidence to understand the structure and any existing naming, retention or ownership rules that materially affect the work.

We prefer the minimum access needed. An inventory, representative screenshots or directory listing may be sufficient.

What is outside the scope?

Bulk migration, destructive deletion, data-retention legal advice, records-management certification, forensic discovery, eDiscovery, penetration testing, recovery of inaccessible or deleted files and organisation-wide information-governance transformation are outside the standard engagement.

Is this the right service?

It is a good fit where one shared area has genuine source-of-truth, duplicate, stale-content, versioning, ownership, retrieval or archive problems.

You may not need it if people can already find the current version reliably, ownership is clear and the problem is mainly cosmetic.

What does House of Carol not promise?

No automatic deletions. No invented retention rules. No unsupported claims about savings. No attempt to turn one messy team drive into an enterprise information-governance programme.
