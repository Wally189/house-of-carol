Shared Drive & Document Cleanup

If “where is the latest version?” is a normal question, your shared document area is doing too much work badly.

For: small organisations where one shared document area has become difficult to navigate, duplicated, stale or dependent on individual memory.

House of Carol reviews one team’s shared document area and gives you a clear source-of-truth model, a simpler target structure and a practical, non-destructive cleanup plan.

£1,250 fixed

One team or business function · one shared document area · one primary platform · remote.

Nothing is deleted, moved or changed without your explicit approval.

When the shared drive stops being a source of truth

You may have duplicate or near-duplicate folders, stale templates that still look current, several files called “final”, inconsistent naming, information split across historical team structures, or one person who knows where everything lives while everybody else has to ask.

The issue is not untidy folders for their own sake. It is uncertainty about where information belongs, who owns it and which copy people should actually use.

What changes

You leave with an agreed way to make the area easier to understand and control: where the source of truth should sit, who should own key information, how files and versions should be named, where common information should live, what should be kept, moved, archived or reviewed, and the order in which to make those changes safely.

This is a bounded review and cleanup plan. It is not a bulk migration, records-management certification or organisation-wide information-governance programme.

What you will receive

Current-state map. A practical view of the main areas, their uses, ownership, duplicate or stale locations and the places where people are most likely to lose time or confidence.

Source-of-truth and ownership recommendations. Clear recommendations for which locations should be authoritative and who should own them, for you to confirm.

Simpler target structure. A proposed folder or library structure, including sensible archive boundaries.

Naming and version rules. Simple rules that help people recognise the current copy without inventing a complicated filing system.

Cleanup plan. A non-destructive keep, move, archive or review sequence with approval points and clear exceptions.

Retrieval map. A straightforward guide to where common information should live and what should not be duplicated.

Implementation checklist and handover. An ordered checklist for making the changes, followed by up to 45 minutes of remote review and clarification.

How it works

1. Bound the area

We confirm the team or function, the shared area, the platform, the named owner and the specific problem you want fixed.

2. Short discovery

We spend up to 90 minutes with the owner and, where useful, representative users to understand how the area is actually used.

3. Map and diagnose

We work from an inventory, representative screenshots or a directory listing where that is enough. Read-only access is used only where it is genuinely necessary.

We identify duplicate, stale or ambiguous locations, inconsistent naming or versioning, unclear ownership, archive uncertainty and structural friction.

4. Design the target and cleanup sequence

We propose the smallest sensible structure, source-of-truth model, naming/version rules and a controlled sequence for keeping, moving, archiving or reviewing material.

5. Check and hand over

We make sure the recommendations are tied to the evidence we actually saw, then review the plan with you and resolve any final clarifications.

What we need from you

We need one named owner who can explain how the area is used and make or obtain the necessary decisions.

We also need the platform or repository, the known problems and constraints, any relevant naming, ownership or retention rules, and enough safe evidence to understand the area.

Where an inventory, screenshots or a directory listing are sufficient, we use those. We prefer the minimum access needed and do not need your passwords or MFA codes.

Is this a good fit?

This is a good fit where one bounded shared area has genuine source-of-truth, duplication, versioning, ownership, retrieval or archive friction and there is a named person able to make the necessary decisions.

It is probably not worth buying if everyone can already find the current version reliably and the main complaint is simply that the folder names look untidy.

When this is not the right service

This is not a bulk migration service and does not include mass deletion or movement, recovery of deleted or inaccessible files, eDiscovery, penetration testing, data-retention legal advice, records-management certification or an organisation-wide information-governance transformation.

High-risk or unusually sensitive information may require a separate assessment before work can continue.
