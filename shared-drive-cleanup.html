Shared Drive & Document Cleanup

If “Where is the latest version?” is a normal question, the system needs sorting.

Shared document areas rarely become a mess all at once.

A new folder appears because somebody cannot find the old one. Another copy is made “just in case”. Projects finish but never quite get archived. Old and current work get mixed together. Nobody wants to move anything because nobody is certain what is still needed.

Eventually, the organisation has plenty of documents and very little confidence about where the right one lives.

House of Carol reviews one shared document area, works out what is actually going on and gives you a simpler structure and a controlled plan for putting it right.

For: SMEs and charities in England and Wales.

When it is useful

This review may be useful when people regularly ask where documents live, several copies appear to be “the latest”, old and current work are mixed together, folder structures have grown around individual habits, onboarding requires a long explanation of where things are hidden, nobody is quite sure who owns the structure, or previous tidy-ups have simply created another folder.

The problem does not need to be dramatic. It needs to be real enough that people no longer trust the shared document area.

What you get

Current-state map

A clear view of the agreed document area, how it is currently organised and the main retrieval, duplication, ownership and control problems.

Source-of-truth and ownership decisions

A practical map showing what should be treated as current or authoritative, where it should live and where an ownership decision is needed.

Duplicate, stale and unclear-content triage

A controlled review of material that appears duplicated, obsolete or uncertain, showing where a decision needs to be made rather than guessing.

Simpler target structure

A proposed folder or library structure for the agreed area that is easier to understand and maintain.

Naming and version rules

Practical conventions for files, folders and versions that people can actually use.

Keep, move, archive and review plan

A non-destructive plan showing what should remain, what is proposed to move, what may be suitable for archive and what requires further review or approval.

Retrieval map

A straightforward guide showing where common types of information should live and where people should look first.

Implementation checklist

A step-by-step route for putting the agreed structure into use.

Handover

A handover session to walk through the proposed structure, controls, open decisions and implementation plan with the people who will use it.

Not a mass delete

House of Carol does not arrive and start deleting folders.

Where something appears stale, duplicated or unclear, we identify it and show the decision that needs making.

The standard review does not include deleting, moving or migrating files, changing permissions or writing changes back into your repository.

If later implementation or migration work is required, that is separate scope and requires separate agreement and authority.

House of Carol does not invent legal retention rules or decide that records may be destroyed when the necessary authority is absent.

How we look at the document area

We normally start with an exported file or folder inventory, directory listing, representative screenshots or another sufficient sample.

Controlled read-only access is used only where the review cannot be completed properly without it.

We use the minimum information needed to understand the problem and avoid opening document contents unless that is necessary for the agreed review.

We do not ask you to send passwords, MFA codes, recovery codes, API keys or equivalent credentials.

If the repository contains unusually sensitive, confidential or regulated material, the access route may need to be reassessed before the review continues.

Who it is for

Owners, operations managers, office and business managers, charity operations leads, governance or administration leads and team leads who are responsible for a shared document area but no longer trust its structure.

The current standard engagement is for organisation-paid work for an organisation established in England and Wales.

Probably not worth buying

If everyone can reliably find the current version, ownership is clear and the real problem is simply that you dislike the folder names, keep your £1,250.

If you simply need somebody to move thousands of files from one place to another, this standard review is not a bulk migration service.

And if the real problem spans the whole organisation, several systems or a specialist regulated information environment, it needs to be assessed separately rather than squeezed into this fixed scope.

What House of Carol does not promise

This review is not legal retention advice or a compliance certification.

It does not guarantee financial savings or guarantee that every document can be classified where the available evidence is incomplete.

Where the evidence does not support a safe decision, the review shows the decision that remains to be made rather than inventing certainty.
