Shared Drive & Document Cleanup

A fixed-fee review for a shared document area that has become difficult to trust.

If people regularly ask which file is current, where something lives or whether an old folder can be archived, the problem is no longer just untidy names. The team has lost a dependable source of truth.

We map the current area, identify the ownership and version problems, design a simpler target structure and give you a controlled plan for putting it right.

For: small and medium-sized organisations and charities with one shared document area that needs a practical reset.

£1,250 fixed fee

One team or business function · one shared document area · one primary platform.

Normally returned within 7 working days once the agreed inputs are complete.

This is a review and controlled cleanup plan, not a bulk migration. We do not delete files or carry out bulk file moves as part of the standard review.

When the shared drive stops being dependable

Recognise the problem

People regularly ask where documents live.

Several copies appear to be “the latest”.

Old and current work are mixed together.

The folder structure has grown around individual habits.

New starters need a long explanation of where things are hidden.

Nobody is quite sure who owns the structure or decides what is current.

What changes

From ambiguity to a controlled plan

The aim is not to produce a prettier folder tree. It is to make the important decisions clear: where current information should live, who owns it, how versions should be handled and what should happen to material that is duplicated, stale or uncertain.

You leave with a simpler target structure and a sequenced plan for moving from the current mess to a more dependable shared area without treating deletion as the default answer.

What you receive

The standard review

Current-state map. The main areas, owners and users, plus duplicate, stale, ambiguous and hard-to-find material.

Source-of-truth and ownership decisions. Clear decisions about what should be authoritative and who is responsible.

Target structure. A simpler proposed folder or library structure with practical naming, version and archive rules.

Cleanup plan. A keep / move / archive / review sequence, including the decisions and stop points that need customer approval.

Retrieval map. A simple guide to where common information should live and how people should recognise the current copy.

Implementation checklist and handover. Ordered actions to put the plan into practice, followed by a remote handover session of up to 45 minutes.

How it works

A bounded review, not an open-ended consultancy project

1. Define the area. We confirm the one team or function, one shared document area and one primary platform that the review will cover.

2. Understand what exists. We use a folder/file inventory, screenshots or directory listing where that is enough. Safe read-only access is used only where it is necessary and agreed.

3. Design the fix. We map the current problems, define source-of-truth and ownership decisions, design the target structure and set out the cleanup sequence.

4. Hand over the plan. We take you through the recommendations, clarify the decisions you need to make and leave you with the implementation checklist.

What we need from you

Enough information to understand the problem — no more than necessary

  • A named owner or decision-maker for the document area.
  • A discovery session of up to 90 minutes about how the area is used and where the problems occur.
  • The current platform and either a sufficient inventory, screenshots or directory listing, or safe read-only access where genuinely needed.
  • Any existing naming, ownership, retention or archive rules.
  • Known constraints, sensitive areas or problem folders that need particular care.

We do not need to read every document simply because it exists. Decisions about the business meaning of files — including whether something should ultimately be retained, archived or deleted — remain with the customer.

Good fit, no fit and boundaries

It should be obvious when this is — and is not — the right service

Good fit

This review is a good fit when one shared area has become difficult to navigate, several copies compete to be current, ownership is unclear, old and live work are mixed together, or previous tidy-ups have not fixed the underlying problem.

Probably not worth buying

If people can already find the current version reliably, ownership is clear and the problem is mainly that you dislike the folder names, keep your £1,250 and tidy it internally.

Outside the standard review

Organisation-wide information-governance programmes, multiple systems, bulk file migration, destructive deletion, legal advice on retention, records-management certification, forensic discovery, eDiscovery, penetration testing and recovery of inaccessible or deleted files are not included.

If the requirement turns out to be materially larger than one team, one shared area or one primary platform, it needs a separate scope rather than being squeezed into the fixed review.

An illustrative example

The kind of problem this review is designed to untangle

Suppose three folders contain similarly named policy documents and nobody can confidently say which one is current. One copy looks newer, another sits in the folder everyone uses, and a third has the word “approved” in the filename.

We would map the copies and their context, identify who is responsible for deciding the authoritative version, define where that source of truth should live, and set out what should be kept, moved, archived or reviewed. We would not delete a file simply because it looks old.

This is an illustrative example, not a customer case study.
