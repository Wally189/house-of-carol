How we work

Good work should be easy to trust.

House of Carol keeps its standards practical: fair terms, useful work, less unnecessary data, accessible design, sensible security and environmental claims we can actually prove.

Customers

Price, scope and important limitations should be understandable before work starts. We do not use fabricated testimonials, fake scarcity, hidden material terms or pressure tactics. Recommendations are recommendations: the customer remains in control of what changes.

Privacy and security

We try not to collect information we do not need. Process evidence should be redacted wherever practical, and sensitive material should not be sent simply because a form makes it possible. Privacy and security are considered when a service or tool is chosen, not added at the end.

Our approach is informed by current guidance from the Information Commissioner’s Office and the National Cyber Security Centre.

Accessibility and service design

We aim for clear language, sensible journeys, keyboard-friendly interfaces, strong contrast and layouts that work across common devices. The GOV.UK Service Standard and WCAG 2.2 AA principles are useful excellence benchmarks for our digital work even where public-sector accessibility rules do not apply to us.

When something goes wrong

We want problems raised early. A complaint should receive a clear acknowledgement, a fair look at the evidence, an explanation and a proportionate remedy where we got something wrong. We use the Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman’s principles of good complaint handling as a voluntary benchmark for openness, fairness and learning.

Suppliers and money

Small businesses know what late payment feels like. House of Carol intends to pay valid supplier invoices promptly and to use clear, fair payment terms. The Office of the Small Business Commissioner’s Fair Payment Code is our benchmark if supplier relationships become material enough to justify formal participation.

Technology and AI

Technology has to earn its place. We use automation or AI where it improves the work without weakening privacy, accuracy, accessibility or appropriate human control. Customer-facing material is reviewed as ordinary professional work; we do not publish prompts, internal model instructions or fabricated machine certainty.

Environment

Our order of work is simple: avoid unnecessary consumption, reduce what remains, measure material impacts, improve them, then make only environmental claims we can substantiate.

That means fewer unnecessary journeys and print runs, longer useful life for equipment and software, less duplicate storage and procurement that considers environmental impact alongside privacy, accessibility, reliability and cost.

House of Carol does not currently claim to be carbon neutral or net zero. If our activity becomes material enough to justify carbon reporting, we will use the current UK Government greenhouse-gas conversion factors and state the boundary and method used. Environmental claims will follow the Competition and Markets Authority’s Green Claims guidance.

Ethics

We will not deliberately profit from deception, exploitation, unlawful discrimination, coercive selling or privacy invasion. House of Carol is a commercial business, but commerce does not require treating people badly. The underlying test is whether the work is lawful, truthful, proportionate, useful and respectful of human dignity.
