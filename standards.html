How we work

Good repair should be easy to trust.

The standards are deliberately less exciting than the shopfront: clear scope, useful work, minimum necessary data, sensible security, accessibility, customer control and claims we can actually prove.

Start with reality

A business repair begins with how the work or customer journey actually behaves, including workarounds and unofficial steps. We protect strengths before removing friction and try simplification before adding technology.

Customers and commercial fairness

Price, scope, important exclusions and material limitations should be understandable before work starts. We do not use fabricated testimonials, fake scarcity, hidden terms or pressure tactics. A customer remains in control of whether recommendations are adopted and whether House of Carol performs any implementation.

Privacy, access and security

We try not to collect information we do not need. Customer evidence should be redacted wherever practical. Passwords should not be sent to the House. Where an agreed repair genuinely needs system access, we prefer customer-executed or guided changes, or least-privilege access supplied by the customer and removed when no longer required.

Our approach is informed by current guidance from the Information Commissioner’s Office and the National Cyber Security Centre.

Implementation and handover

Consequential digital, website, document or workflow changes are tested and, where relevant, have an appropriate backup or rollback route before cutover. The customer authorises consequential adoption. The House aims to hand back editable artefacts, understandable operating information and customer control rather than create artificial lock-in.

Accessibility and service design

We aim for clear language, sensible journeys, keyboard-friendly interfaces, strong contrast and layouts that work across common devices. GOV.UK service-design principles and WCAG 2.2 AA principles are useful voluntary excellence benchmarks even where public-sector accessibility duties do not apply to House of Carol.

Evidence and results

Assumptions are labelled. Quantified savings or performance improvements are not invented. Fictional demonstrations stay labelled as fictional. Named or identifiable customer case studies require permission and, where before/after numbers are shown, the measurement and its limitations should be clear.

When something goes wrong

We want problems raised early. A complaint should receive a clear acknowledgement, a fair look at the evidence, an explanation and a proportionate remedy where the House got something wrong. We use the Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman’s principles of good complaint handling as a voluntary benchmark for openness, fairness and learning.

Technology and AI

Technology has to earn its place. We use automation or AI where it improves the work without weakening privacy, accuracy, accessibility or appropriate human control. Customer-facing material is reviewed as ordinary professional work; we do not publish prompts, internal model instructions or fabricated machine certainty.

Environment

Our order of work is simple: avoid unnecessary consumption, reduce what remains, measure material impacts, improve them, then make only environmental claims we can substantiate. House of Carol does not currently claim to be carbon neutral or net zero.

Ethics and boundaries

House of Carol is commercial, but it will not deliberately profit from deception, exploitation, coercive selling or privacy invasion. It also declines work that crosses into legal, tax, regulated financial, clinical, immigration or another professional area it is not competent and authorised to provide.
