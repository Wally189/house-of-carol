Demonstration · not client work

The Friday
receipt scramble.

A fictional example showing what happens when one ordinary small-business process has grown by accident.

◆ No real customer, testimonial or claimed saving is represented here.

As found

The process technically works.

Supplier invoices arrive by email. Receipts appear on paper or a phone. Purchases are typed into a spreadsheet. The bank statement is checked. Missing information is chased. A pack is assembled for the accountant. Every step is individually reasonable; together they create repeated handling.

01

Evidence arrives everywhere

Email inbox, paper receipts and phone photos become three different places to remember.

02

The same facts are typed again

Supplier, date, amount and purpose are copied into a separate spreadsheet even when the source already contains most of them.

03

The bank becomes a detective tool

The statement is used to reconstruct what happened because evidence and purpose were not captured cleanly at the time.

04

Friday becomes catch-up day

The owner spends an evening matching, chasing and packaging information instead of simply checking exceptions.

The breakpoint

Not an accounting
software problem.

The core defect is fragmented capture and duplicate handling. Buying another app before agreeing the accountant’s actual minimum input could simply add a fourth place for the same information.

BETTER STATE

Capture once. Check exceptions. Hand off what is actually needed.

  • confirm the accountant/bookkeeper’s minimum required format first;
  • choose one evidence-capture route;
  • record the few fields that are genuinely missing at the point of purchase;
  • use one authoritative preparation register only if a register is actually required;
  • use the bank statement as a completeness check rather than the main reconstruction tool;
  • review exceptions weekly; and
  • send the accountant the agreed package, not every intermediate working file.

Do first

Three moves before buying anything.

01

Ask the accountant one question

What is the minimum information and evidence you actually need from us, and in what form?

02

Pick the front door

Choose one capture route for invoices and receipts and stop maintaining parallel evidence piles.

03

Kill duplicate recording

Keep a separate spreadsheet only for fields or controls that are not already provided elsewhere.

04

Then judge technology

If the simplified process still has repetitive work worth automating, technology finally has something coherent to automate.
