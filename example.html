House of Carol / One-Process Rescue / Demonstration

Fictional demonstration · not client work

The Friday
receipt scramble.

A successful little business. Sensible individual habits. One recurring process that has grown into a Friday-night reconstruction exercise.

◆ No real customer, testimonial, measured saving or claimed result is represented here.

The symptom

“Why am I still sorting this out on Friday night?”

Supplier invoices arrive by email. Receipts appear on paper or a phone. Purchases are typed into a spreadsheet. The bank statement is checked. Missing information is chased. A pack is assembled for the accountant. Nothing is individually absurd. The combined process is.

01 · Capture

Evidence arrives in three places.

Email, paper and phone photos become three separate things to remember and revisit.

02 · Recording

The same facts get typed again.

Supplier, date, amount and purpose are copied into a spreadsheet even when the source already contains most of them.

03 · Checking

The bank statement becomes detective work.

It reconstructs what happened because evidence and purpose were not captured cleanly at the time.

04 · Catch-up

Friday becomes the control system.

The owner matches, chases and packages information after the week’s actual work has finished.

The real breakpoint

Not an accounting
software problem.

The defect is fragmented capture and duplicate handling. Buying another app before understanding the accountant’s actual requirements could simply create a fourth place for the same information.

BETTER STATE

Capture once. Check exceptions. Hand off what is actually needed.

  • ask the accountant/bookkeeper what information and evidence is genuinely required;
  • choose one evidence-capture route;
  • record only genuinely missing information at the point it is easiest to know;
  • keep one preparation register only if one is actually needed;
  • use the bank statement as a completeness check, not the main reconstruction tool;
  • review exceptions rather than rebuilding everything; and
  • send the agreed hand-off package, not every intermediate working file.

Do first

Three changes before anybody buys software.

This is where House of Carol should earn its keep: make the problem smaller before making the solution larger.

Now

Ask, choose, remove.

  1. Ask: what does the accountant actually need and in what form?
  2. Choose: one front door for evidence.
  3. Remove: duplicate recording that serves no separate control purpose.

Only then judge whether any remaining repetitive work is worth automating.

What would The Fix mean here?

Turn the answer into working artefacts.

If this fictional customer wanted implementation help, a separately scoped Fix might produce the agreed capture method, preparation register if needed, a simple weekly exception check and a short hand-off SOP. The exact design would depend on the accountant’s real requirements and the tools the business already owns.

That is an illustration of the implementation route, not a promise that every Rescue requires or qualifies for implementation.
