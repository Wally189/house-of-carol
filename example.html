House of Carol / Workbench / Receipt scramble

Workbench ticket 001 · fictional demonstration

The Friday
receipt scramble.

A successful little business. Sensible individual habits. One recurring process that has quietly turned Friday night into reconstruction work.

◆ No real customer, testimonial, measured saving or claimed result is represented here.

Lift the bonnet

Nothing here is individually stupid.

Supplier invoices arrive by email. Receipts appear on paper or a phone. Purchases are typed into a spreadsheet. The bank statement is checked. Missing information is chased. A pack is assembled for the accountant. The problem only appears when you follow the whole journey.

01 · Capture

Evidence arrives in three places.

Email, paper and phone photos become separate things to remember and revisit.

02 · Recording

The same facts get typed again.

Supplier, date, amount and purpose are copied into a spreadsheet even when the source already contains most of them.

03 · Checking

The bank statement becomes detective work.

It reconstructs what happened because evidence and purpose were not captured cleanly at the time.

04 · Catch-up

Friday becomes the control system.

The owner matches, chases and packages information after the week’s actual work has finished.

The breakpoint

Not an accounting
software problem.

The defect is fragmented capture and duplicate handling. Buying another app before understanding the accountant’s actual requirements could simply create a fourth place for the same information.

WHAT THE HOUSE NOTICES

The hand-off was never designed as one thing.

  • evidence and purpose separate too early;
  • the same transaction is represented in several places;
  • memory bridges the gaps between them;
  • the bank is asked to reconstruct history rather than check completeness; and
  • the accountant’s actual minimum requirement is unknown, so the business maintains an assumed preparation process.

Repair before technology

Ask, choose, remove.

This is where a process review earns its keep: make the problem smaller before making the solution larger.

The first three moves cost no software.

Now
  1. Ask: what does the accountant actually need, and in what form?
  2. Choose: one front door for evidence.
  3. Remove: duplicate recording that serves no separate control purpose.

Only then judge whether any remaining repetitive work is worth automating.

BETTER STATE

Workbench conclusion

Capture once. Check exceptions. Hand off what is actually needed.

The redesigned process starts with the accountant’s minimum requirement, chooses one evidence route, records only genuinely missing information at the easiest point, and uses the bank as a completeness check rather than the main reconstruction tool.

If the customer wanted The Fix

Turn the diagnosis into working pieces.

A separately scoped Fix might produce the agreed capture method, a preparation register only if genuinely needed, a simple weekly exception check and a short accountant hand-off SOP. The exact design would depend on the accountant’s real requirements and the tools the business already owns.

That is an illustration of the implementation route, not a promise that every Rescue requires or qualifies for implementation.
