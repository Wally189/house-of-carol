The problem

When the problem is between the steps

Most service friction is not one spectacular failure. It is the accumulation of small breaks between people, channels and decisions.

Customers are asked for the same information twice. Work waits in an inbox. Different channels give slightly different instructions. Ownership becomes unclear. Staff compensate with chasing, workarounds and manual fixes.

This review follows one journey from beginning to end, including the work happening behind the scenes, so those issues can be seen together rather than treated as unrelated incidents.

The outcome

What changes

You finish with one evidence-tagged view of the current journey: what the customer experiences, what staff and systems do behind the scenes, where hand-offs occur and which controls genuinely need to remain.

Friction, waiting, duplication, unclear ownership and service failures are separated from assumptions. Where the evidence is weak, the gap is shown rather than filled with guesswork.

You also receive a short set of practical priorities — normally up to five improvements. Where the evidence supports it, that includes a bounded target-state journey.

The work

What you will receive

Six useful outputs, all focused on one defined customer journey.

Journey scope and evidence view

The customer type, journey start and end, channels, roles and evidence used, with assumptions and unknowns identified.

Current-state journey and service map

One end-to-end view of the customer-facing steps and the backstage work, hand-offs, decisions, controls and material systems supporting them.

Friction and service-failure analysis

An evidence-based view of waiting, repeated information, chasing, lost context, unclear ownership, inconsistent messages and duplicated work.

Evidence gaps

Anything that cannot currently be established is marked clearly, together with the evidence that would help resolve it.

Priority improvements

Normally up to five proportionate improvements, with the reasoning, dependencies and practical next step made clear.

Target state and handover

Where the evidence supports it, a bounded future journey and a practical handover showing what can change locally and what needs separate work.

The process

How it works

1. Confirm the boundary

Agree the customer segment, journey start and end, accountable service owner, channels involved and the important controls that must be understood.

2. Discovery

A remote discovery session, normally around 60 minutes, is used to understand how the service currently works and clarify the evidence supplied.

3. Map and review

Map the current journey from customer-facing steps through the relevant backstage work, then identify material friction, failures, hand-off problems, duplication and evidence gaps.

4. Prioritise

Turn the findings into a small set of practical priorities. Produce a target-state journey only where the evidence justifies one.

5. Handover

Explain the findings in a remote readout of up to 45 minutes. One bounded correction cycle is included for factual corrections within the agreed scope.

Fit and boundaries

What to know before the review

What needs to be in place?

One accountable service owner; one defined customer segment; and one journey with a clear start and end.

We need the material current customer-facing steps, forms, messages and channels; significant internal hand-offs and procedures; known approvals and controls; and relevant existing operational or customer evidence where available.

The standard review does not require passwords, MFA codes, administrator credentials, unrestricted live-system access or unnecessary raw personal data.

What is outside the scope?

This is not a whole-business transformation programme, primary customer research, CRM or website implementation, a full UX/UI redesign, or a legal or regulatory compliance audit.

The review does not implement recommended process, staffing, communications, system or technology changes. Necessary legal, financial, privacy, security, accessibility, safeguarding or quality controls are not removed simply to make the journey look smoother.

Is this the right service?

It is designed for a small service organisation, or one coherent service line, where one important customer journey crosses several people, systems or channels and there is no reliable end-to-end view of what is happening.

If the real problem is a single broken internal process, a technology implementation, broad governance redesign or another specialist piece of work, a more focused service may be more appropriate.

Where the work would require large volumes of identifiable customer information, special-category data, safeguarding judgement, regulated professional decisions or privileged live-system access, the standard review stops and the appropriate specialist route must be considered first.

What does House of Carol not promise?

The review does not guarantee improvements in NPS, CSAT, retention, conversion, revenue, cost or cycle time.

Where evidence is incomplete, the gap is identified rather than turned into an unsupported conclusion.
