Customer Journey & Service Operations Review

See the whole customer journey, separate evidence from assumptions, and identify practical improvements without stripping away controls that genuinely matter.

When a service crosses different people, inboxes, forms, systems or channels, each part can make sense on its own while the overall customer journey becomes slow, repetitive or difficult to follow.

For: Small UK service businesses, or one coherent service line within them, with one important end-to-end customer journey to review.

£1,250 fixed fee

The standard review covers one customer segment, one journey from an agreed start event to an agreed end state, up to three customer-facing channels and up to six material internal roles or hand-offs.

Planned delivery: normally within seven working days after a complete input pack and discovery session.

No invented customer voice · necessary controls stay visible · no implementation bundled into the review · no guaranteed NPS, retention, conversion or revenue improvement

What changes

You finish with one evidence-tagged view of the journey from the customer's starting need to the agreed end state — including what happens behind the scenes.

The review makes waiting, repeat information, unclear ownership, inconsistent messages, duplicated work, avoidable transfers and service failures visible.

It also distinguishes genuine friction from approvals, safeguards or other controls that need to remain.

If the evidence is strong enough, the review shows a bounded target-state journey. If it is not, the correct output is an evidence plan rather than invented certainty.

What you will receive

Journey scope & evidence legend

A clear boundary for the customer type, journey start and end, channels, roles, evidence supplied, assumptions and unknowns.

Current-state journey & service map

The customer-facing steps and the backstage work, hand-offs, decisions, controls and material systems that support them.

Friction & service-failure analysis

An evidence-tagged view of repeated information, waiting, chasing, lost context, unclear ownership, inconsistent messages, duplicated work and other material service problems.

Evidence gaps

Claims that cannot currently be substantiated are marked as unknown or hypothesis, with the smallest useful evidence needed to improve the decision.

Priority improvements

Normally up to five proportionate improvements, each with rationale, evidence level, owner or dependency notes, control implications and the smallest sensible next action.

Target state & handover

A bounded target-state journey where evidence warrants one, plus a practical handover explaining what can be changed locally and what needs separate process, technology, research or specialist work.

Before anything is agreed, House of Carol confirms the journey boundary, available evidence, known mandatory controls and whether the work fits the standard review. If it does not, the work is declined, narrowed or routed rather than stretched beyond the service.

What needs to be in place?

One accountable service owner who can explain the service and make decisions about next steps.

One defined customer segment and one journey with a clear start and end.

Current customer-facing steps, messages, forms, channels, important internal hand-offs and known approval or control points.

Existing operational or customer evidence where available. Direct customer research is not required for the standard review, and customer sentiment is not invented where evidence is absent.

No password, MFA code, admin credential, unrestricted live-system access or unnecessary raw personal data is required for the standard review.

What is outside the scope?

The standard review does not conduct customer interviews, surveys, focus groups or mystery shopping.

It does not implement the recommended process, system, staffing, communications, CRM, helpdesk or website changes.

It is not a full UX/UI redesign, enterprise transformation programme, legal or regulatory compliance audit, or substitute for specialist professional advice.

Necessary legal, financial, privacy, safeguarding, accessibility, security or quality controls are not removed simply to make a journey appear smoother.

It does not guarantee improvements in NPS, CSAT, retention, conversion, revenue, cost or cycle time.

Is this the right review for our organisation?

It is designed for a small service organisation where one important customer journey crosses several people, inboxes, forms, systems or channels and nobody currently has a reliable end-to-end view.

It is a good fit when the organisation can provide enough existing evidence to map the real journey and wants a bounded independent review before committing to implementation.

You may need a different service if the main issue is one broken internal process, document control, management information, a support knowledge base, broad governance redesign, primary customer research or technology implementation.

If the work requires large volumes of identifiable customer data, special-category information, safeguarding judgement, regulated professional decisions or privileged live-system access, the standard review pauses for the appropriate specialist route.
