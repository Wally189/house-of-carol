Customer Support Knowledge Base Build

Turn repeated support questions into clear, consistent knowledge your team can actually use.

When the same questions keep coming back, inconsistent answers create unnecessary work and make it harder for staff and customers to know what is correct.

We organise your approved product and service information into a structured support knowledge base, with clear ownership, a consistent answer standard and a defined route for questions that cannot safely be answered from the available information.

For: SMEs dealing with recurring customer questions where useful knowledge is spread across documents, messages or individual staff experience.

£1,750 standard · indicative scope £1,250–£2,500 · remote delivery

One agreed support domain, normally up to 30 material source items and up to 15 priority articles or approved answers.

We normally plan around seven working days from complete, approved source material and confirm the delivery date before work begins.

When the answer depends on who gets the question

This service is for SMEs dealing with recurring customer questions where useful knowledge is spread across different documents, messages or individual staff experience.

You may find that answers vary between people, nobody clearly owns the information, older guidance remains in circulation, or difficult questions have no obvious escalation route.

The aim is not to create more documentation. It is to give your team a clearer, maintainable source of approved support knowledge.

What changes

Your priority support questions are brought into one agreed structure.

Staff have a consistent way to find and use approved answers. Questions that are uncertain, disputed or outside the available evidence have a clear escalation route rather than being guessed at.

Responsibility for reviewing and updating the content is made explicit, so the knowledge base has an owner after the project ends.

What you receive

Source and content inventory

A clear view of the material currently relied on for the support area in scope.

Support taxonomy

A practical structure for organising questions, topics and answers so information can be found more easily.

Priority support content

Up to 15 priority articles or approved answers within the standard scope, built from the information your organisation has approved.

Answer and article standard

A consistent format your team can continue using when support content is added or revised.

Escalation and no-answer routes

Clear handling for questions where the answer is unknown, disputed, incomplete or needs specialist judgement.

Ownership and review arrangements

Named responsibility, review roles and triggers for checking whether support content remains current.

Maintenance process and handover

A practical method for updating, correcting and retiring content, together with the information your team needs to maintain the knowledge base after handover.

Final factual correction

One bounded correction cycle for agreed factual or scope errors before the project closes.

How it works

1. Agree the support area

We confirm the product, service or coherent support domain in scope and identify the person who can approve the underlying facts.

2. Review the sources and recurring questions

We identify the material your team currently relies on, where answers come from and where information conflicts or is missing.

3. Design the structure and answer standard

We establish the taxonomy, article format and rules for handling uncertain or unanswered questions.

4. Build the priority knowledge content

We organise the agreed content and create the priority articles or approved answers within scope.

5. Set ownership and hand over

We agree review and update responsibilities, complete the bounded factual correction cycle and hand the maintainable structure to your team.

What we need from you

You will need to provide the material your organisation currently relies on to answer the support questions in scope.

You will also need one accountable person who can confirm what is correct when sources are incomplete or inconsistent and approve the final support content.

Timely access to those sources and decisions matters. The delivery timetable begins from the point that the agreed source material is complete and available for us to work from.

Is this a good fit?

This service is designed for an SME that answers the same customer questions repeatedly and wants a clearer, more maintainable way of managing the answers.

It is particularly relevant where staff rely on different sources, answers vary depending on who responds, ownership is unclear or difficult questions do not have a reliable escalation route.

When is this not the right service?

It is not intended as a replacement support platform, a chatbot deployment, a complete redesign of your customer-service operation or an unlimited content-migration exercise.

A substantially larger knowledge library or more complex systems requirement needs separate scoping.

What is outside the standard scope?

We do not invent product facts, policies, promises or eligibility decisions where your source material does not provide an answer.

We do not replace legal, regulatory or other specialist judgement. We do not publish your support content without your approval.

Chatbot or AI deployment is not included unless separately scoped.

Ongoing House of Carol-managed maintenance after handover is also not included in the standard project. The maintenance method and ownership arrangements are handed over to your organisation.

Built from information your organisation can stand behind

Your organisation remains the authority for its product and service facts.

Where sources conflict, are stale or do not answer a question, we make that visible and route it for a decision rather than filling the gap with an assumption.

A useful support knowledge base is not simply a collection of answers. It needs a clear source, a clear owner and a clear response when the answer is genuinely uncertain.
